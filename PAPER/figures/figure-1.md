# Figure 1 — method-overview variants (prompt source for image/diagram tools)

**Purpose.** Text descriptions of candidate designs for the paper's Figure 1 (the method overview,
`fig:overview` in `paper/sections/03_methodology.tex`). Each variant below is written to be pasted into
an image-generation or diagramming tool as the core of a prompt, then refined. A programmatic **draft**
of Variant A currently occupies the figure slot (`paper/figures/method_overview.pdf`, generated by
`paper/figures/make_figures.py`) so the manuscript compiles; the production figure replaces it 1:1.

**What Figure 1 must communicate (all variants, non-negotiable):**
1. Two editing modes under one objective: **trim** moves recorded pickups (demand); **lift** reroutes a
   trajectory's final seeking states (supply moves with them).
2. The asymmetry that motivates lift: trim alone **levels down** (the under-served side is untouched);
   lift **provides** service rather than redistributing it.
3. Edits are small and bounded (a two-cell ball; the tapered tail blends back into the original route —
   no teleporting).
4. The supply gradient as a *map*: lift targets the cells where added taxi presence most improves
   fairness.

**House constraints:** print-safe (must read in grayscale — encode meaning in shape/position, color as
reinforcement only); one accent color maximum (the current draft uses a blue accent for everything
lift/supply-related); no photorealism; no text smaller than ~6.5 pt at a 7-inch width (`figure*`,
double-column); the words "pillar" and any product/tool names must not appear; keep labels short —
the caption carries the sentences.

---

## Variant A — "Three panels, one city" (the current draft's design; safest)

A horizontal strip of three identical stylized city grids (~10×8 cells, thin light gridlines),
left to right, each with a short title above.

- **Panel 1 — "the service gap."** In the upper-left region, a dense cluster of small open squares
  (taxi presence) with a few × marks (pickups) among them — label "over-served." In the lower-right
  region, many × marks with a single open square — label "under-served." A tiny two-symbol legend
  (square = taxi presence, × = pickup) in the panel's empty corner.
- **Panel 2 — "trim: relocate excess pickups."** Same city. Two or three of the over-served cluster's
  × marks shown ghosted (light gray) at their original positions with short solid arrows (≤2 cells
  long) pointing to their new positions just outside the hotspot. The under-served region is pixel-identical
  to Panel 1 — visibly untouched. A quiet gray annotation near it: "levels down: under-served untouched."
- **Panel 3 — "lift: reroute seeking tails."** Same city. A soft single-hue glow (the accent, ~12%
  opacity) over the under-served region — labeled "value of added presence (supply gradient)." One
  driver trajectory drawn as a solid polyline entering from the upper edge; its **last four segments**
  bend smoothly into the glow (drawn dashed in the accent color, arrowhead at the end, the final ×
  landing inside the glow). The bend should look like a gentle detour, not a right angle — the taper.
- **Reading order cue:** panels separated by generous whitespace; optional thin arrows between panel
  titles (1 → 2 → 3).

*Why this variant:* mirrors the paper's argument order (problem → old mode → new mode); every claim in
the caption points at one panel. Weakness: three small panels compress detail.

## Variant B — "Before/after split city" (single panel, maximal simplicity)

One wide panel; the same city drawn twice, left half "before," right half "after," separated by a thin
vertical divide with a large arrow crossing it.

- **Left (before):** the service gap exactly as Variant A Panel 1.
- **Right (after):** both edits shown *simultaneously* on the after-city: two short trim arrows
  relocating pickups out of the hotspot AND one accent-dashed tail bending into the (now-glowing)
  under-served region. Under-served region gains: the rerouted tail's presence (one new open square
  drawn in accent outline) + its landed pickup.
- Two tiny callouts with leader lines: "trim (demand moves)" on the hotspot arrows; "lift (supply
  moves with the tail)" on the bent trajectory.

*Why this variant:* strongest at-a-glance gestalt ("the method turns the left city into the right
city"); best for talks. Weakness: loses the leveling-down teaching moment (trim's failure mode is not
shown in isolation).

## Variant C — "Mechanism ribbon" (pipeline diagram, most technical)

A left-to-right ribbon of four stages connected by arrows, each stage a small box with a micro-diagram:

1. **"Real corpus 𝒯"** — a stack of thin polylines (trajectories), one highlighted.
2. **"Attribution"** — TWO small heat-maps side by side inside one box: "deficit (where unfairness
   lives)" with hot cells in the over-served cluster, and "value of presence (∂fairness/∂supply)" with
   hot cells in the under-served region. This is the variant that visualizes the paper's
   two-attribution contribution explicitly.
3. **"Bounded edits"** — one grid showing both moves at once: a trim arrow (≤2 cells) and a tapered
   tail bend; a small dashed square around each edit marking the two-cell ball.
4. **"Upweighted imitation"** — a training icon: the edited trajectories drawn thicker (weight w) than
   the rest of the stack, feeding a small policy box; a subscript "×w on the edited slice."

*Why this variant:* the only one that shows the full method including the downstream recipe (edit →
upweight), and it foregrounds the two attribution maps. Weakness: busier; needs careful sizing to stay
legible at column scale; the city-level fairness story is implicit.

## Variant D — "A + gradient inset" (recommended target for the production figure)

Variant A's three panels, plus one refinement: Panel 3's glow is rendered as an actual smooth
**heat-map field** (single-hue, light→dark with intensity = supply-gradient value) rather than a flat
tint, with 2–3 faint iso-contours. Optionally, Panel 2 gains a miniature inset in its empty corner: a
tiny bar pair showing "over-served ↓, under-served —" (trim) that Panel 3's twin inset answers with
"over-served ↓, under-served ↑" (trim+lift). The insets encode the ablation table's qualitative story
directly in the figure.

*Why this variant:* keeps A's argument structure, adds the one element image models render beautifully
(a smooth field) and the one element reviewers remember (the paired insets). Weakness: the insets must
stay schematic — no axis numbers, or they'll be read as data.

## Prompt-support notes (for any tool)

- Style words that match the draft: *"clean vector schematic, thin gray gridlines, generous white
  space, academic paper figure, monochrome with a single blue accent, no photorealism, no 3D, no
  drop shadows."*
- Symbols: taxi presence = small open square; pickup = ×; trajectory = thin polyline; reroute = dashed
  accent polyline with a small arrowhead; edit bound = small dashed square (two cells).
- The under-served region should read as *starved of squares*, not as empty — keep its × marks dense.
- Aspect ratio: ~7:2.2 for the three-panel strip (`figure*` full width); ~7:3 if Variant D's insets
  are included.
- Everything the figure claims must stay qualitative — no numbers inside the figure; the caption and
  §4's tables carry the quantities.
