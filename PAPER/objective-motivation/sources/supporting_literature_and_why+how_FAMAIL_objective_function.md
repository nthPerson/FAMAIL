# Supporting Literature and "Why + How" Narrative for the FAMAIL Objective Function

## TL;DR
- **FAMAIL's objective is defensible and well-anchored in established literature**: each of its three terms and its editor map cleanly onto foundational work — Frisch–Waugh–Lovell partial regression and "conditional statistical parity" for the demographic term, transportation-equity Gini indices for the spatial term, the ST-SiameseNet/trajectory-user-linking line for the fidelity discriminator, FGSM/iFGSM plus algorithmic-recourse for the constructive editor, and Kamiran–Calders pre-processing/reweighing for the augmentation pairing.
- **The novelty is combinatorial, not atomic**: no single component is unprecedented, but four specific compositions have no clear prior (candidate novelty claims flagged below) — chiefly the FWL-partial-R²-on-demand-residualized-service objective, and repurposing bounded ε-L∞ adversarial perturbation as a fair trajectory-editing operator.
- **The leveling-down finding is a strength, not a liability**: framed with egalitarian ethics (Parfit; Temkin) and fair-ML results (Mittelstadt et al. 2023; Zietlow et al. 2022; Pinzón et al. 2022), "over-service reduction under a frozen-supply constraint" reads as a principled constrained optimum with a clear supply-side future direction, pre-empting the strongest reviewer objection.

## Key Findings
1. **F_causal's construction is the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem applied to fairness.** Residualize-then-project is a pure linear-algebra identity (Frisch & Waugh 1933; Lovell 1963), so "share of demand-adjusted service variance explained by demographics" is principled — but it is *associational*, not causal, and the "causal" name should be retired (rename to F_demo).
2. **Demand-adjustment before attributing disparity has a direct fairness precedent**: "conditional statistical parity" (Corbett-Davies et al. 2017), which restricts disparities to those *not* explained by permitted/legitimate factors.
3. **Gini over supply-normalized service is standard transportation-equity practice** (Hörcher & Graham 2021; Karner et al. 2024), but Gini measures dispersion not level — a property that itself invites the leveling-down critique and argues for Theil/Atkinson robustness checks.
4. **The frozen identity discriminator is grounded in ST-SiameseNet/HuMID (KDD 2020) and the broader TUL literature**, and freezing (vs. a live GAN adversary) is justified by well-known GAN instability/mode-collapse.
5. **Repurposing iFGSM constructively is legitimized by algorithmic recourse** (Ustun et al. 2019; Wachter et al. 2017): the same bounded, minimal, signed-gradient perturbation tooling is already used to prescribe beneficial changes. The ε-L∞ bound reinterpreted as an identity-preservation budget is a clean conceptual transfer.
6. **Temperature-annealed soft discretization is textbook Gumbel-softmax/straight-through** (Jang et al. 2017; Maddison et al. 2017; Bengio et al. 2013).
7. **The closest applied neighbor, Zheng et al. (2023)**, documents and fixes demand underestimation in disadvantaged neighborhoods — but via *in-processing* on prediction; FAMAIL's *pre-processing on demonstrations for imitation* is a distinct and defensible niche.

## Details

### Executive framing — the "why + how" thesis
Demand/dispatch models learned by imitation from historical taxi trajectories inherit and can amplify demographic service inequity, because the demonstrations they clone encode where service historically concentrated. FAMAIL therefore intervenes at the data level — the pre-processing family (Kamiran & Calders 2012; Feldman et al. 2015) — but under a hard realism constraint that generative approaches violate: synthesizing "fair" trajectories risks distributional collapse and rewrites the already-fair majority, a failure mode paralleling Zietlow et al.'s (2022) finding that synthetic-data/heuristic fairness fixes are frequently Pareto-inefficient. FAMAIL instead *edits* a small, attribution-targeted slice of real trajectories. Its objective decomposes into a primary demographic-fairness term operationalized as a partial-R² via the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem after adjusting for legitimate demand (the transportation analogue of conditional statistical parity, Corbett-Davies et al. 2017), a secondary Gini-based spatial-equity term drawn from transportation-equity practice, and a frozen trajectory-identity discriminator (ST-SiameseNet lineage) regularizing edits toward realism. The edit repurposes bounded ε-L∞ adversarial-perturbation machinery (Goodfellow et al. 2015; Kurakin et al. 2017) constructively, made differentiable over a discrete grid by temperature-annealed soft assignment (Jang et al. 2017). Finally, edited demonstrations are upweighted so fairness survives behavior cloning rather than being averaged away — instance reweighing (Kamiran & Calders 2012) transplanted to imitation learning.

### F_causal — demand-adjusted demographic fairness (PRIMARY term)

**Supporting literature.** The construction "residualize the outcome and the protected attributes on a legitimate covariate, then regress" is exactly the partial-regression identity of the **Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) theorem** (Frisch & Waugh, *Econometrica* 1933; Lovell, *JASA* 1963; anticipated by Yule 1907). FWL guarantees that FAMAIL's Stage-2 coefficients on demographics after partialling out demand equal the demographics' coefficients in the full multiple regression, so the F_causal partial-R² is a principled "share of demand-adjusted service variance explained by demographics." Crucially, FWL is a "pure linear-algebra fact without any probabilistic assumptions on the data generating process" — i.e., it concerns *conditional association*, not causation. Conceptually, adjusting for a *legitimate* factor (demand) before attributing residual disparity to a protected attribute is the fairness notion of **conditional statistical parity** (Corbett-Davies et al., KDD 2017), defined there as "the notion that the probability the action is taken is the same across groups, conditional on certain permitted characteristics (e.g. previous arrests)"; the same paper cautions that "conditional statistical parity requires that one define the 'legitimate' factors ℓ(X), and this choice significantly impacts results." Using "how well the protected attribute is predicted from the rest of the data" as the fairness quantity is the logic of **Feldman et al. (2015)**, who linked disparate impact to the predictability (balanced error rate) of the protected class. Positioning against demographic parity/disparate impact draws on **Verma & Rubin (2018)** and **Barocas, Hardt & Narayanan (2023)**.

**How it motivates FAMAIL (drafted paragraph, paper-ready).** Raw demographic parity would flag any neighborhood whose service differs from the mean as "unfair," but taxi service legitimately tracks demand: a business district *should* see more pickups than a sleepy residential block. FAMAIL therefore measures fairness only in the component of service that demand does *not* explain. We first fit a flexible power-basis map from per-unit demand to the supply-to-demand ratio and take the residual; we then ask how much of that residual is explained by neighborhood demographics. By the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem, this residualize-then-project construction recovers the same demographic association as a full multiple regression controlling for demand, so F_causal = 1 − r²_demo is an interpretable "proportion of demand-adjusted service inequity attributable to demographics." This mirrors conditional statistical parity (Corbett-Davies et al. 2017): we quotient out a legitimate factor and restrict only the illegitimate, demographic component. The idempotency of the centering and hat matrices additionally yields exact per-unit attribution, localizing *where* unfairness concentrates and selecting which pickups to edit — a fairness-as-predictability logic in the spirit of Feldman et al. (2015).

**Contrast / novelty.** Conditional statistical parity is normally a *binary-classifier* constraint on predicted labels; FAMAIL transplants it to a *continuous, spatial, supply/demand-ratio outcome* and turns the residual partial-R² into a differentiable *objective* with exact per-unit attribution used for editing. I found no prior work using an FWL-based partial-R² of demographics on a demand-residualized service ratio as an editable fairness objective in transportation — a candidate novelty (flagged as an unverified absence, not a proof of nonexistence).

**Reviewer risks + rebuttals.**
- *"The 'causal' term isn't causal."* Concede explicitly: F_causal is associational — a partial R² of a cross-sectional OLS on observational demographics, with no identification or counterfactual. FWL is an algebraic identity about conditional association. Rename to F_demo.
- *"Ecological fallacy."* Demographics resolve to 10 district-level profiles, so associations are ecological and few-DOF; report as a limitation and avoid individual-level claims.
- *"Why not demographic parity?"* Unadjusted parity penalizes legitimate demand variation and mislocates the edit; conditional parity is the recognized remedy (Corbett-Davies et al. 2017).

### F_spatial — spatial service equity (SECONDARY term)

**Supporting literature.** The **Gini coefficient** is standard for distributional (in)equity of transport service; recent transportation-equity work uses Gini, Theil, and Atkinson side by side (**Karner, Pereira & Farber 2024**, *Transportation*, "Advances and pitfalls in measuring transportation equity"; **Hörcher & Graham 2021**, "The Gini index of demand imbalances in public transport," *Transportation*). The inequality-measurement literature (Atkinson 1970; Theil 1967; De Maio 2007) documents when each index is preferred — notably **Theil's decomposability** into between-/within-group components and **Atkinson's** tunable inequality-aversion parameter. A known limitation: Gini (and CoV, Theil) measure dispersion, not level, so they can prefer "equally poor" to "unequally better" distributions (Karner et al. 2024). Supply-normalized service rates as the equity variable follow the demand-imbalance framing of Hörcher & Graham (2021).

**How it motivates FAMAIL (drafted paragraph).** Beyond demographic fairness, we want service to be spatially even relative to the taxi supply actually present. We include a Gini-based term over supply-normalized service rates — pickups per active taxi and dropoffs per active taxi — where a lower Gini means service is spread more evenly across active cells. Gini is the most widely used and interpretable inequality index in transportation-equity research (Hörcher & Graham 2021; Karner et al. 2024), and a differentiable pairwise formulation lets it enter the gradient objective directly. Because F_spatial is demographic-independent, it acts as a general spatial-smoothness regularizer complementing the demographic F_causal term.

**Contrast / novelty.** The contribution is not the metric but its *differentiable pairwise* form embedded in a gradient-based editor. The choice of Gini over Theil/Atkinson is defensible but not dominant.

**Reviewer risks + rebuttals.**
- *"Why Gini, not Theil/Atkinson?"* Gini is standard, interpretable, and parameter-free; Theil is group-decomposable and Atkinson encodes inequality aversion (Karner et al. 2024). Since FAMAIL already isolates the demographic axis in F_causal, Theil's decomposability advantage is partly redundant here — a defensible reason to keep Gini, while reporting Theil/Atkinson as robustness alternatives.
- *"Gini rewards leveling down."* Acknowledge directly: dispersion-only indices can prefer uniformly lower service (Karner et al. 2024). This connects to §3 and is a reason F_spatial carries the low weight (0.2) and is paired with the fidelity constraint.

### F_fidelity — realism regularizer

**Supporting literature.** The frozen discriminator is a driver-conditioned three-stream Siamese network in the **ST-SiameseNet** line (**Ren, Pan, Li, Zhou & Luo**, "ST-SiameseNet: Spatio-Temporal Siamese Networks for Human Mobility Signature Identification," *KDD 2020*), which frames **Human Mobility Signature Identification (HuMID)** — verifying whether trajectories were generated by a claimed agent. This sits within the **trajectory-user linking (TUL)** literature (Gao et al. 2017, TULER; Zhou et al. 2018, TULVAE; Miao et al. 2020, DeepTUL), which establishes that mobility patterns are highly user-identifying. Using an identity model as a *fidelity* criterion parallels standard evaluation of generated trajectories by distributional realism — Jensen–Shannon divergence over radius of gyration, trip length, and visit-frequency statistics (widely used across mobility-generation benchmarks, e.g., Feng et al. 2020 and successors). The frozen (non-adversarial) design is motivated by well-documented instability/mode collapse of live GAN adversaries; Ho & Ermon (2016) note the GAN analogy in imitation learning.

**How it motivates FAMAIL (drafted paragraph).** An edit that improves fairness but produces trajectories no real driver would generate is useless for training a realistic demand model. We regularize edits with a frozen driver-identity discriminator from the ST-SiameseNet/HuMID family (Ren et al. 2020): because individual mobility signatures are highly identifying (the premise of the entire TUL literature, from TULER onward), a trajectory that still "reads as" the same driver after editing has preserved human fidelity. We freeze the discriminator — trained once, never updated during editing — to avoid the instability and mode collapse of a live adversarial game, so it supplies a stable realism gradient and a backstop against large edits. A separate Jensen–Shannon divergence check on aggregate mobility statistics guards against generator-style distributional collapse, following standard mobility-generation evaluation practice.

**Contrast / novelty.** Novel *use*: ST-SiameseNet was built for driver verification/robustness; FAMAIL repurposes a frozen instance as a fairness-editing realism regularizer. Because edits are tiny, its gradient w.r.t. the edited pickup is ≈0 — retained as a guardrail rather than an active driver, an honest and slightly unusual design point.

**Reviewer risks + rebuttals.**
- *"If its gradient is ~0, why keep it?"* As a distributional backstop and to certify the ε-bound is doing the fidelity work; the JS-divergence check is the operative collapse guard.
- *"A frozen discriminator can be gamed."* True in principle; the bounded ε-L∞ edit (below) limits exploitation more than the discriminator does.

### The editor — attribution-guided ST-iFGSM with soft differentiable discretization

**Supporting literature.** The editor adapts the **iterative Fast Gradient Sign Method**: FGSM (**Goodfellow, Shlens & Szegedy**, *ICLR 2015*, arXiv:1412.6572) and its iterative/basic-iterative variant (**Kurakin, Goodfellow & Bengio**, *ICLR 2017 Workshop*, arXiv:1607.02533). The spatio-temporal instantiation is the project group's own **ST-iFGSM** (*KDD 2023*, "ST-iFGSM: Enhancing Robustness of Human Mobility Signature Identification Model via Spatial-Temporal Iterative FGSM"). Repurposing adversarial-perturbation machinery *constructively* has clear precedent in **algorithmic recourse / counterfactual explanations** (Wachter, Mittelstadt & Russell 2017; **Ustun, Spangher & Liu 2019**; Karimi et al. 2020/2021), which generate minimal, bounded, *actionable* input changes to flip an outcome favorably — structurally identical to a bounded, targeted "beneficial perturbation." The minimal-perturbation/imperceptibility principle transfers directly to identity-preservation. Differentiating through the discrete grid uses **Gumbel-softmax/Concrete** relaxations (**Jang, Gu & Poole**, *ICLR 2017*; **Maddison, Mnih & Teh**, *ICLR 2017*) and **straight-through estimators** (**Bengio, Léonard & Courville 2013**), with temperature annealing bridging continuous exploration to discrete assignment. Gaussian/kernel smoothing of spatial histograms to obtain gradients is standard for gridded spatial data.

**How it motivates FAMAIL (drafted paragraph).** To edit pickups we must move probability mass on a discrete grid in the direction that most improves the objective, under a hard "stay realistic" budget. Adversarial-example methods solve almost exactly this problem in reverse: FGSM and iterative FGSM (Goodfellow et al. 2015; Kurakin et al. 2017) compute the minimal signed-gradient perturbation that changes a model's output, bounded in L∞ so the change is imperceptible. FAMAIL inverts the sign of the intent — the same ε-L∞-bounded signed-gradient step now moves a pickup toward *greater* fairness, capped at ε = 2 grid cells so a pickup can never drift outside the driver's identity signature no matter how many iterations run. This constructive reuse mirrors algorithmic recourse (Ustun et al. 2019; Wachter et al. 2017), where the same counterfactual-perturbation tooling prescribes minimal beneficial changes. Because the grid is discrete but gradient ascent needs differentiability, we make cell counts differentiable via Gaussian smoothing over a 5×5 neighborhood (matching how active-taxi supply is aggregated), with a temperature τ that anneals from broad early exploration to precise late assignment — the soft-to-hard schedule of Gumbel-softmax and straight-through relaxations (Jang et al. 2017; Bengio et al. 2013). Smoothing over the whole grid collapses the signal, so the 5×5 window is the design sweet spot; τ is exposed as a controllable continuous↔discrete knob.

**Contrast / novelty.** Two defensible novelties: (1) using an *adversarial-attack* method as a *fairness data-editing* operator on spatio-temporal mobility (recourse edits individual feature vectors to flip a decision; FAMAIL edits real trajectory pickups to reduce population-level demographic inequity); (2) temperature-annealed soft cell assignment with a 5×5 supply-matched smoothing window as an explicit, tunable discretization bridge. Reinterpreting the ε-bound as an *identity-preservation* budget rather than an attack-stealth budget is a clean conceptual transfer.

**Reviewer risks + rebuttals.**
- *"iFGSM is just an attack — this is a gimmick."* The math (bounded signed-gradient ascent on a differentiable objective) is method-agnostic; recourse research already legitimizes constructive reuse (Ustun et al. 2019).
- *"Gaussian smoothing biases the counts."* It is a controlled relaxation annealed toward the true discrete assignment (Jang et al. 2017); report sensitivity to τ and window size.
- *"Is ε=2 arbitrary?"* Tie it to the empirical spatial scale of the driver signature and the supply-aggregation window; plot fidelity/JS-divergence vs. ε.

### Downstream pairing — fair data augmentation with upweighting

**Supporting literature.** FAMAIL is a **pre-processing** fairness intervention (edit the data, then train any model) — the family of **Kamiran & Calders (2012)** (suppression, massaging, reweighing) and **Feldman et al. (2015)** (disparate-impact removal). Upweighting edited demonstrations is **instance reweighing for fairness** (Kamiran & Calders 2012) transplanted to **imitation learning/behavior cloning**. The downstream model FAMAIL protects is the taxi-imitation lineage: **Ho & Ermon**, "Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning," *NeurIPS 2016* (Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 29), pp. 4565–4573 (arXiv:1606.03476) → **cGAIL** (Zhang, Li et al., "cGAIL: Conditional Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning — An Application in Taxi Drivers' Strategy Learning") and the explainable variant **xGAIL** (Pan et al., *KDD 2020*). The closest applied neighbor is **Zheng et al. (2023)**, "Fairness-enhancing deep learning for ride-hailing demand prediction," *IEEE Open Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems*, vol. 4, pp. 551–569 (DOI:10.1109/OJITS.2023.3297517): on Chicago TNC data their socially-aware SA-Net plus bias-mitigation regularization "reduces the MPE [mean percentage prediction error] gap between black and non-black communities by 67% without compromising overall prediction accuracy" (and improves MAE by 2.3% over state-of-the-art), documenting exactly the systematic demand underestimation in disadvantaged neighborhoods that FAMAIL's data edit targets upstream.

**How it motivates FAMAIL (drafted paragraph).** Editing the unfair slice is necessary but not sufficient: if we simply retrain by behavior cloning on the edited dataset, the small fair slice is averaged away and the model relearns the old bias — a genuine null we verify empirically. We therefore upweight the edited demonstrations during downstream training so the intervention propagates into the learned policy rather than being diluted. This is instance reweighing for fairness (Kamiran & Calders 2012) transplanted to imitation learning: where reweighing classically breaks the label–protected-attribute association, here it ensures the edited (fairer) demonstrations dominate the cloned policy. We verify against two controls — random reweighting and "select-already-fair" reweighting — isolating that it is *edit-specific* upweighting, not reweighting per se, that recovers fairness. This addresses the demand-model bias quantified by Zheng et al. (2023) in ride-hailing, but intervenes on the training data rather than adding an in-processing regularizer.

**Contrast / novelty.** FAMAIL combines pre-processing *editing* with *edit-targeted upweighting* inside an *imitation-learning* pipeline — a combination I found no direct precedent for (candidate novelty). Most fairness-in-transportation work (e.g., Zheng et al. 2023) is in-processing (regularizers) on *prediction*, not pre-processing on *demonstrations for imitation*.

**Reviewer risks + rebuttals.**
- *"Why not in-processing like SA-Net?"* Pre-processing is model-agnostic and reusable across any downstream imitation learner, and keeps the intervention auditable at the data level.
- *"Upweighting is ad hoc."* It is grounded in reweighing (Kamiran & Calders 2012) and validated against random and select-already-fair controls.

### The leveling-down framing

A structural analysis found — and proved for the demand-only/frozen-supply problem — that fairness improves *only* by reducing over-service to advantaged areas, never by lifting service to under-served areas. Framing this honestly with egalitarian-ethics and fair-ML literature converts a potential weakness into a principled, well-positioned contribution.

**Philosophical grounding.** The **leveling-down objection** to egalitarianism originates with **Derek Parfit** ("Equality and Priority," *Ratio* 10(3), 1997, 202–221; the original 1991 Lindley Lecture is titled "Equality or Priority?" — the titles differ, so cite carefully). Parfit argues that if equality is achieved only by making the better-off worse off with no gain to anyone, it seems no improvement in any respect. **Larry Temkin** (*Inequality*, OUP 1993; "Equality, Priority, and the Levelling Down Objection," in *The Ideal of Equality*, 2000, 126–161) defends non-instrumental egalitarianism against this, rejecting the person-affecting "Slogan." Citing both signals that FAMAIL understands *why* leveling down is normatively troubling.

**Algorithmic-fairness grounding.** **Mittelstadt, Wachter & Russell (2023)**, "The Unfairness of Fair Machine Learning: Levelling Down and Strict Egalitarianism by Default" (*Michigan Technology Law Review* 30(1); arXiv:2302.02404), find that fairness measures cause "'levelling down,' where fairness is achieved by making every group worse off, or by bringing better performing groups down to the level of the worst off," and propose "'leveling up' systems by enforcing minimum acceptable harm thresholds, or 'minimum rate constraints,' as fairness constraints at the design stage." **Zietlow et al. (2022)**, "Leveling Down in Computer Vision: Pareto Inefficiencies in Fair Deep Classifiers" (*CVPR*, 10410–10421; arXiv:2203.04913), show empirically that common fairness heuristics degrade the worst-off group too, and that only one strategy escaped it: "we propose an adaptive augmentation strategy that, uniquely, of all methods tested, improves performance for the disadvantaged groups" — a direct parallel to FAMAIL's data-augmentation stance. Formal results tighten this: **Pinzón, Palamidessi, Piantanida & Valencia (2022)**, "On the Impossibility of Non-trivial Accuracy in Presence of Fairness Constraints" (*AAAI* 36(7), 7993–8000), prove that for some distributions an equal-opportunity constraint forces accuracy down to trivial levels — leveling down can be *constraint-forced*, not an optimizer failure.

**How FAMAIL positions itself (drafted paragraph).** We show — empirically and structurally — that the current editor improves demographic fairness only by reducing over-service to advantaged neighborhoods, never by raising service to under-served ones. We argue this is the *constrained optimum* of a demand-only, frozen-supply problem, not an optimizer bug: (i) residual-variance attribution selects over-served cells; (ii) the demand lever is near-inert on the under-served side (demand floor + weak gradient); and (iii) true inequity is on the supply side, but supply (active-taxi count) is frozen, so the editor can only move pickups. An oracle bound shows any demand-only editor could raise the under-served group only by *deleting* its recorded pickups — perverse. Leveling down is therefore the boundary solution the problem geometry permits, and we make this explicit. This mirrors the fair-ML consensus that leveling down reflects design choices, not inevitability (Mittelstadt et al. 2023), and can be constraint-forced (Pinzón et al. 2022). Following Mittelstadt et al.'s "leveling up" prescription and Zietlow et al.'s (2022) finding that augmentation was the one strategy that helped the disadvantaged, our future direction is a **supply-side lever** — editing/augmenting active-taxi supply, not just demand — which our analysis shows is the only mechanism that can raise service to under-served areas. Normatively, even over-service reduction has defensible egalitarian standing (Parfit 1997; Temkin 1993), but we do not rest the contribution there.

Do not submit with these unverified.

**Stage 2 — pre-empt objections in-text.**
- Add a short "on the choice of Gini" sentence naming Theil (decomposable) and Atkinson (aversion parameter) as alternatives (Karner et al. 2024), and report a Theil/Atkinson robustness check.
- Add the leveling-down subsection using Parfit/Temkin + Mittelstadt et al. 2023 + Zietlow et al. 2022 + Pinzón et al. 2022, framing over-service reduction as a constrained optimum with the supply-side lever as future work.
- Report editor sensitivity to τ, smoothing-window size, and ε (fidelity/JS-divergence curves).

**Stage 3 — sharpen the novelty claims.** State the four candidate novelties (below) as "to our knowledge" claims, not absolute firsts. Position against Zheng et al. (2023) explicitly as pre-processing-for-imitation vs. in-processing-for-prediction.

**Benchmarks that would change these recommendations.**
- If a reviewer or a targeted search surfaces a prior FWL-partial-R² fairness objective on residualized outcomes, downgrade novelty claim #1 to "we adapt."
- If the JS-divergence collapse check fails at the chosen ε, the frozen discriminator's ~0 gradient becomes a liability — reintroduce a (still non-adversarial) fidelity gradient or tighten ε.
- If the "select-already-fair" control matches edit-specific upweighting, the augmentation claim collapses to ordinary reweighing — report and reframe.

## Caveats
- **Two citations remain unverified** (cGAIL venue/year; ST-iFGSM KDD 2023 author list) — flagged above; confirm manually.
- **Parfit title ambiguity is real**: the *Ratio* 1997 article is registered as "Equality and Priority"; the 1991 Lindley Lecture is "Equality or Priority?" Use the exact title matching the edition you cite.
- **Karner et al. (2024)** print-issue year may appear as 2025 in some indices; the DOI (10.1007/s11116-023-10460-7) is confirmed. **Barocas–Hardt–Narayanan** exists as a free 2019 web edition (fairmlbook.org) and a 2023 MIT Press book — cite the version you use.
- **TUL secondary citations** (TULER/TULVAE/DeepTUL, and the "Feng et al. 2020" JS-divergence mobility-evaluation reference) were confirmed as a body of work but their exact page numbers were not each individually verified; author/year/venue are reliable, page ranges should be checked.
- **Novelty claims are "absence of found precedent," not proof of nonexistence.** The four candidate novelties — (1) FWL-partial-R²-on-demand-residualized-service objective; (2) iFGSM repurposed as a fair trajectory-editing operator; (3) pre-processing edit + edit-targeted upweighting for imitation; (4) differentiable pairwise Gini with supply-matched 5×5 smoothing + temperature-annealed soft assignment — should be stated as "to our knowledge."
- **Pinzón et al. (2022)** proves impossibility for *equal opportunity in classification*, not for FAMAIL's exact objective; use it as an analogy for "fairness constraints can force leveling down," not as a direct theorem about the demand-only editor. FAMAIL's own oracle/structural bound is the load-bearing formal claim and should be presented as such.
- A very recent **Wilms & Heitz (FAccT 2026)** Pareto-frontier characterization and an unrefereed SSRN preprint (Yang et al., "Fairness May Backfire") exist on when fairness need/needn't level down; both fall outside the requested 2015–2025 peer-reviewed window and should be treated as forward-looking pointers, not anchors.
