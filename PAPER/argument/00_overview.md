# Overview — the FAMAIL argument in one page

## The problem

Real urban-mobility data encodes demographic service inequity: where taxis actually pick up and drop
off is correlated with neighborhood wealth, income, and migrant/hukou population structure. Demand
models learned from that data by imitation inherit — and can amplify — the bias. We want a way to
make the *data* fairer along a demographic axis while keeping it realistic, and to make that fairness
survive into a model trained on it.

## The approach — edit the unfair slice, don't generate

FAMAIL is a fairness-oriented **data-augmentation** method. Instead of generating synthetic
trajectories (which risks distributional collapse and loss of human fidelity), it **edits a small,
targeted slice of the real trajectories**: per-(cell, time) attribution identifies where demographic
unfairness concentrates, and a signed-gradient step relocates the affected pickup cells within a tiny
ε = 2 ball. Then, in downstream training, the edited demonstrations are **upweighted** so their
fairness propagates rather than being averaged away.

## The result — two pillars

1. **The edited data is the fairest *faithful* source.** It is fairer (higher F_causal) than the raw
   data and than data generated by behavior-cloning (BC) or GAN models, while remaining
   identity-faithful (Fidelity-A unchanged from raw). Editing beats generating.
2. **Vanilla training averages the edit away; upweighting recovers it — edit-specifically.** A vanilla
   driver-conditioned BC policy trained on the edited data is *not* fairer (null transfer). But
   **upweighting** the edited demonstrations recovers the fairness with a clean monotone dose-response
   — and control arms that upweight a *random* subset or the *already-fairest* trajectories do **not**
   reproduce it, so the gain is driven by the edit, not by oversampling or selection.

## External validity

The entire argument was re-run on an independent city (San Francisco Cabspotting) with **no algorithm,
metric, or fidelity change**. Every directional conclusion reproduced; the weighted-BC recovery and
its control contrasts were, if anything, *sharper*. Shenzhen is the primary dataset; SF is external
validity. Because F_causal is a city-specific, associational measure, SF is reported as *reproducing*
Shenzhen, not as beating it.

## Headline numbers

Shenzhen uses the PRIMARY `{housing, comp, migrant}` feature set; all values are seed means.
`F_causal` is **associational** (1 = fairest; a rename to `F_demo` is a pending PI decision).
`p = 0.03125` is the n = 6 Wilcoxon sign-unanimity floor — the weighted-BC evidence rests on the mean
Δ + t-CIs + monotone dose-response + null/negative controls (see [`04_evaluation.md`](04_evaluation.md)).

| result | Shenzhen (primary) | SF (external validity) |
|---|---|---|
| **Editor** ΔF_causal (causal-emphasis) | **+0.0144** (0.7988 → 0.8132) | **+0.0139** (0.8752 → 0.8891) |
| **Pillar 1** — edited = fairest faithful source (F_causal) | edited **0.8132** > raw 0.7988 ≈ bc 0.7980 | edited **0.8891** > raw 0.8752 ≈ bc 0.8789 |
| **Realism at the edit** | Fidelity-A ≈ raw (identity preserved) | F_fidelity **0.968**; Fidelity-A 0.958 = raw |
| **L2** — vanilla transfer (edited − raw) | **−0.0012** (null) | **+0.0004** (null) |
| **Pillar 2** — weighted-BC recovery @ w30 | **+0.0311** (6/6, t-CI excludes 0) | **+0.0387** (6/6) |
| **Controls** (random / most-fair select) | ~null | negative (sharper) |
| **Model-level variance** | −0.0011 ± 0.0032 (null) | −0.0005 ± 0.0043 (null) |
