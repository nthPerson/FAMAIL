sketches the pipeline; its three coarse steps (attribute, edit, upweight)
give the order of the five blocks below, each of which answers one
challenge of \S\ref{sec:overview}, named in its opening sentence.

\subsection{The Collective Fairness Objective}\label{sec:objective}

Challenge C3 is a requirement on the objective itself: what the editor ascends
must be collective rather than per-trajectory, must credit demand for the
service it explains, and must be differentiable and exactly decomposable into
local units, so a corpus-level disparity can be attributed to the trajectories
that carry it (\S\ref{sec:attribution}). FATE edits trajectories by gradient
ascent on one objective combining demographic fairness, spatial equity, and
driver fidelity.
% (T9 lever a: "differentiable in the coordinates an edit moves" folded into
% "differentiable" — the coordinates clause is restated verbatim three lines
% below, "exact gradients flow through Y to the coordinates being edited".)

\textbf{Demand-adjusted demographic fairness ($F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, primary).}
Fairness here cannot mean equal service everywhere: taxi service legitimately
tracks demand (C3). We therefore measure fairness only in the component of
service that demand does not explain (the logic of conditional statistical
parity \cite{corbettdavies2017}, which restricts disparities to those not
explained by permitted factors). Stage one fits a four-term power-basis curve
$g_0(D)$ from per-unit demand to the service ratio and takes the residual
$R_i = Y_i - g_0(D_i)$: the part of each unit's service that demand does not
account for. Stage two asks how much of that residual the demographics
explain, as the coefficient of determination $r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}}$ of a
regression of $R$ on the z-scored demographics. Fairness is the unexplained
share,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fdemo}
F_{\mathrm{demo}} \;=\; 1 - r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}} ,
\end{equation}
the fraction of demand-adjusted service variation that demographics cannot
predict: $F_{\mathrm{demo}} = 0$ means demographics fully explain who is
over- and under-served (fully unfair), and $F_{\mathrm{demo}} = 1$ means
they explain none of it (fully fair). The curve $g_0$ is fitted once on the
recorded corpus and then held fixed, and stage two is evaluated in closed
form through its projection (hat) matrix
\cite{hoaglinwelsch1978}, so the measure is smooth, exact gradients flow
through $Y$ to the coordinates being edited, and every edit step re-fits the
demographic regression exactly, with nothing trained inside the editing loop.

Two important caveats. First, $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ (\emph{demo}
for demographic dependence) is \emph{associational}: the
partial $R^2$ of a cross-sectional regression on observational
district-level demographics, with no identification and no counterfactual.
With about ten district profiles on Shenzhen the association is ecological,
and we make no in\-di\-vid\-ual-level claims. Second, adjusting for demand treats
recorded demand as exogenous; but demand recorded where service has
historically been thin is itself suppressed by that under-service. We return
to this in \S\ref{sec:leveling}, where the same assumption bounds
what a demand-only editor can achieve.
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/MOTIVATION.md (associational caveat; demand endogeneity)

\textbf{Spatial equity ($F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$, secondary).}
The construction adapts Su et al.~\cite{su2018taxigini}, who score every
zone of Shenzhen by its arrival and departure service rates and measure
the city's spatial inequality as the Gini coefficient of each rate. FATE forms the same pair
of rates per active unit, normalized by local taxi presence: the
\emph{departure-service ratio} $\mathrm{DSR}_i = D_i / S_i$ (pickups per
unit of taxi presence) and the \emph{arrival-service ratio}
$\mathrm{ASR}_i = A_i / S_i$ (dropoffs per unit). $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$
is one minus the mean of their two Gini indices; the closed form is in
Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}. Being
demographic-independent, the term regularizes toward spatial equality,
complementing $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$.
% src (lineage): su2018taxigini (Su, Fang, Xu & Huang, IJGI 7(6):230, 2018)
% §4.1-4.2 — per-TAZ arrival/departure service rates + a Gini coefficient per
% rate, on Shenzhen taxi trajectories (Robert drew the ASR/DSR formulation
% from this paper during objective design; PDF supplied 2026-07-24). FATE's
% adaptation: rates per active unit, normalized by local taxi presence S_i
% instead of fleet size x zone area x days; fairness = 1 - mean Gini.
% DSR expansion corrected 2026-07-24 ("demand-service" -> "departure-service"
% per the source; formula D_i/S_i unchanged; code keys + RESEARCHER_HANDOFF
% keep the legacy expansion — mapping in paper/README.md conventions).

\textbf{Fidelity guardrail ($F_{\mathrm{fidelity}}$).} An edit that improves
fairness by producing trajectories no real driver would generate is useless
for training a realistic imitation model. Because individual mobility signatures
are highly identifying \cite{gao2017tuler,zhou2018tulvae,miao2020deeptul}, we score
fidelity with a frozen driver-identity discriminator of the ST-SiameseNet
family \cite{ren2020stsiamese}: an edited trajectory that still reads as its
driver has preserved human fidelity. It is trained once, never updated during
editing, and has near-zero gradient under FATE's small bounded edits, so it
acts as a fidelity \emph{guardrail} rather than a driver of edits;
identity-axis stability under both phases is reported empirically as
\emph{Fidelity-A} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). The
distributional fidelity axis is scored separately at evaluation as
\emph{Fidelity-B} (\S\ref{sec:exp-data-level}).
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/MOTIVATION.md (F_fidelity guardrail framing)

\textbf{Scalarization.} The three terms combine linearly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \alpha_{\mathrm{sp}} F_{\mathrm{spatial}}
+ \alpha_{\mathrm{de}} F_{\mathrm{demo}}
+ \alpha_{\mathrm{fi}} F_{\mathrm{fidelity}},
\qquad \alpha = (0.1,\; 0.8,\; 0.1).
\end{equation}
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/weight-sensitivity/ (EXTENDED_FRONTIER.md + DECISION.md, 2026-07-11)
The weights are selected \emph{empirically}: a sweep with fidelity fixed at
0.1, scored across all three metric classes (\S\ref{sec:exp-setup}), selects
$(0.1, 0.8, 0.1)$ by a three-class criterion, maximizing
$\Delta F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ subject to $\Delta F_{\mathrm{spatial}} \ge 0$
\emph{and} a supply-channel lift-up that stays significant under both
accounting conventions; the full sweep, with the flatness and monotone
lift-up evidence behind the criterion, is in \S\ref{sec:exp-robustness}.
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/weight-sensitivity/EXTENDED_FRONTIER.md (flatness, monotone lift-up, criterion)

\subsection{Attribution under an Edit Budget}\label{sec:editor}

\label{sec:attribution}%
Challenge C1 is a spending problem: at most $k \ll |\mathcal{T}|$
trajectories may change, and the objective is optimized one trajectory at a
time, so each editing phase carries its own selection mechanism.
\emph{Demand deficit attribution} locates where unfairness concentrates and
drives trim; \emph{supply-gradient attribution} locates where added taxi
presence would most improve fairness and drives lift.
% 2026-07-24 (T4): the retired three-step opener ("The editor runs in three
% steps ...") is superseded by the leading paragraph and the block headings; its
% two-mechanism sentence survives here — the claim that must never collapse into
% one ranked list feeding both editors (analysis_C_claims.md C-6, C-7).

\textbf{Demand deficit attribution (trim's selection).} The explained share
$r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdemo} splits exactly across the
active units: each carries a defined share of the
fairness deficit, and the shares sum to the whole with nothing lost or
double-counted (Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}); a signed variant
separates over- from under-served units. Trim takes the trajectories whose
recorded pickups land in the highest-deficit units.
% 2026-07-24 (Robert): "Because the projections ... are idempotent" retired
% from the body (plain-language mandate); the idempotency justification
% lives in App A ("Because M and I-H are idempotent", per-unit partition).
% src: PAPER/argument/03_fairness_theory.md (per-cell attribution)

\textbf{Supply-gradient attribution (lift's selection).} The second mechanism
asks of every active unit: \emph{how much fairer would the city's service be
with marginally more taxi presence here, at this hour?} That quantity is the
gradient of the objective with respect to supply. We
evaluate the objective at hypothetically augmented supply $S + \Delta S$ and
read $\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial S_i$ by automatic differentiation at
$\Delta S = 0$: the marginal fairness effect of added presence at each of the
$N \approx 34{,}500$ active units, in one backward pass. The result is a
\emph{value-of-presence} map over cells and hours: large where added presence would
most repair the fairness terms, and small or negative where presence is
already abundant. Its $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ component has a closed form, given
with its autograd verification in Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §4.1 (autograd-verified closed form)
To turn the map into candidates, a linearized-offset screen ranks trajectories
by the best bounded translation of their tail, a tail being the pickup plus up
to four seeking states immediately before it (Appendix~\ref{app:editor});
${\approx}80$k of the corpus's ${\approx}95$k trajectories are long enough to
carry a movable tail. The screen favors trajectories that already nearly serve
the right areas, and it only \emph{nominates}: each nominee's tail is then
translated under the full objective.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/data/oracle.json (n_trajectories_len_ge3 80,427 of n_trajectories_total 95,297);
% LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §3 (tail = last min(TAIL_LEN=4, len-2) seeking states + the pickup, states[-1]);
% §4.2 (the screen only nominates; full screen detail in app:editor)

\textbf{Spending the budget.} Trim selects by demand
deficit attribution; lift fills the remaining budget with positive-score
nominees
(on the primary city, $k = 10{,}000$ splits into 2{,}455 trim and 7{,}545 lift
edits), and the supply gradient is computed on the post-trim state.
% src: LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §4.3, §5 (budget split, phase order)
Attribution is therefore not a post-hoc explanation of an edited corpus: it is
the mechanism that decides which trajectories are modified by the editor.

\subsection{Outcome-Side and Resource-Aware Editing}

Challenge C4 warns that measured fairness can improve while the under-served
gain nothing, and on our data a demand-only editor does exactly that. FATE
therefore edits through two channels.

\textbf{Outcome-side editing (the \emph{trim} operation).} Trim changes the
measured allocation statistic without changing the process that provides
service. Each selected pickup relocates within the $\varepsilon$-ball,
padding recorded demand into over-served, supply-rich cells so their measured
over-service falls, with supply frozen throughout.
% src: PAPER/argument/03_fairness_theory.md (per-cell attribution; relocation
% within the eps-ball) + restructure/ALGORITHM_FACTS.md §Editor (supply FROZEN
% during trim; outcome-side/resource-aware pairing per ZHANG_DRAFT_DELTA)

\textbf{Why demand-only editing cannot help the
under-served.}\label{sec:leveling} On its own this lever only redistributes
service among better-served areas. On the primary city, every one of the
2{,}455 pickups edited by the demand-only editor originated in advantaged
cells, and not one landed in a disadvantaged cell.
% src: PAPER/external-metrics/LEVELING_DOWN_MECHANISM.md (2,455/2,455 flow analysis; pre-supply-lift doc) — corroborated at alpha*: PAPER/supply-lift/data/a10/shz_trimonly_a10_external_fairness.json (mean(Y|disadv) 7.0734 -> 7.0734 exactly)
The pattern is analogous to the classic leveling-down objection to
equalization \cite{parfit1997}, formalized for machine-learning fairness with
a prescription to level \emph{up} instead \cite{mittelstadt2024}; in a study
of fair image classifiers, data augmentation was the one strategy that helped
the disadvantaged group rather than pulling others down \cite{zietlow2022}.

The analogy is inexact in one
instructive way: trim \emph{relocates} pickups under conservation rather than
removing service, so nothing is destroyed; yet the under-served group is
not helped at all.

Three structural facts make this the constrained optimum of the demand-only
problem rather than an optimizer artifact: selection, leverage at the demand
floor, and a supply-side inequity no demand edit can touch
(Appendix~\ref{app:editor}).

Recorded demand is also not exogenous, which unifies the editor's limitation
with the metric's: where service has been thin, demand is suppressed and
latent (the feedback-loop pathology documented for other public-allocation
systems \cite{ensign2018,lumisaac2016}), so the same censoring that hides
inequity from a demand-adjusted metric leaves a demand-only editor nothing to
move in under-served areas. The one non-perverse lever is the numerator:
$\partial Y_i / \partial S_i = 1 / \max(D_i, d_0)$ is positive everywhere, and
$\Delta Y = 2\,\Delta S$ at the demand floor. Raising under-served service
therefore requires adding taxi \emph{presence} by rerouting real seeking
behavior into under-served cells.
% src: LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §1-2 (dY/dS; oracle gate G0 covered in §4.4 + App. C)

\textbf{Resource-aware editing (the \emph{lift} operation).} Lift changes the
provision process itself. It translates a nominated tail: the pickup moves by
the full offset $\delta$, earlier tail states by linearly tapered fractions of
it, and the anchor state before the tail never moves, so the edit physically
routes the trajectory through the under-served area before the pickup without
teleporting. The moved states carry supply with them, differentiably,
contributing presence to $\Delta S$ over the same $5{\times}5$ neighborhood.
Supply is thus \emph{endogenous}: the optimizer sees the supply consequences
of its own moves and is rewarded for providing service where it improves
fairness. The fidelity term scores the actual rerouted tail at every
iteration, and each completed edit updates a shared running state, so later
edits optimize against the results of previous edits.
Appendix~\ref{app:editor} gives the taper weights, supply floor, and further
editor detail.

necessity. Lift runs after trim and never alters trim's edits, so trim
behaves identically with or without lift: the demand-only results above carry

versus trim+lift ablation
(\S\ref{sec:exp-ablation}) is caused by lift alone. A unified single-pass editor is a natural extension we
leave as future work.

\subsection{Validity- and Fidelity-Constrained Editing}\label{sec:phys-validity}

Challenge C2 makes every edit a constrained edit: an edited trajectory must
remain a record of the same driver's behavior and a physically realizable
path. Two limits bound the intervention: the corpus-level budget $k$ caps how
many trajectories may change, and the per-trajectory bound $\varepsilon$ caps
how far any one of them may move.

Both phases repurpose bounded adversarial
perturbation \cite{goodfellow2015fgsm,kurakin2017ifgsm}, in its
spatio-temporal instantiation \cite{hu2023stifgsm}, \emph{constructively},
in the spirit of algorithmic recourse
\cite{ustun2019recourse,wachter2018counterfactual}.
% 2026-07-25 (review finding #16 page budget): the gloss ": the same
% counterfactual-perturbation tooling that finds a minimal input change to flip a
% decision instead prescribes a minimal, bounded, beneficial one" is dropped here
% because \S\ref{sec:related} states the same idea in its own words ("a minimal
% bounded change prescribed for benefit rather than attack") with the same five
% citations. The constructive framing and every citation survive in both places.
Per
iteration, the edit offset takes a signed-gradient step under a cumulative
$L_\infty$ clip,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step}
\delta \leftarrow \mathrm{clip}\bigl(\eta \cdot
\mathrm{sign}(\nabla_{\delta}\,\mathcal{L}),\, -\varepsilon,\, \varepsilon\bigr),
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{total}} \leftarrow
\mathrm{clip}(\delta_{\mathrm{total}} + \delta,\, -\varepsilon,\, \varepsilon),
\end{equation}
so no pickup ever moves more than $\varepsilon = 2$ cells from its recorded
location, regardless of iteration count: the $\varepsilon$-bound
reinterpreted as an identity-preservation budget rather than an attack-stealth
budget. A temperature-annealed soft cell assignment keeps the discrete cell
counts differentiable in the edit offsets, so gradients flow from the
objective back to the coordinates being edited (Appendix~\ref{app:editor}).
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/MOTIVATION.md (editor); PAPER/argument/03_fairness_theory.md

\textbf{Physical validity.} Edits are held to the same king-move rule the
source trajectories are filtered on (consecutive states move at most one
cell per axis), enforced by an exact backward-reachability repair
(Appendix~\ref{app:editor}). Infeasible edits are not applied: lift skips
them in-loop, and the trim edits with no compliant repair are reverted to the
originals post hoc under the same rule
(\S\ref{sec:exp-data-level} reports the fraction and when it was applied).
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/data/a10/shz_a10_metrics.json (n_reverted_infeasible_trim 118/2,455 ≈ 4.8%);
% PAPER/supply-lift/FINDINGS.md §8 (rule-first disclosure)

The resulting pipeline is the same in both phases: propose a gradient edit,
clip it to $\varepsilon$, discretize and repair continuity (skipping or
reverting the edit if no compliant repair exists), evaluate the objective's
fairness and fidelity terms and keep the best iterate, commit the accepted
edit to the shared corpus state, and repeat until the budget is spent.
% src: restructure/ALGORITHM_FACTS.md §Validity (the six-step pipeline, kept in
% that order so no step reads as a fidelity accept/reject gate).
Accept, skip, and revert are decided by validity, never by a fidelity
threshold: the frozen discriminator is a weighted term in $\mathcal{L}$ at
every iteration and an evaluation-time gate on the finished corpus
(\emph{Fidelity-A}), certifying identity-level behavioral fidelity rather than
full trajectory realism, with the remaining distributional cost disclosed as
\emph{Fidelity-B} (\S\ref{sec:exp-data-level}).

\subsection{Edit-Aware Weighting}\label{sec:downstream}

Challenge C5 is why editing alone is not sufficient: the edited slice is by
design roughly a tenth of the corpus, and a vanilla
behavior-cloning objective that weights every demonstration equally averages
it away and relearns the original bias. We verify this null empirically
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
% (derived: 10,000 / 95,297 ≈ 10.5%; src: PAPER/baselines/demographic-oversampling/FINDINGS.md corpus count)
FATE therefore \emph{upweights} the edited
demonstrations in the imitation loss: instance reweighing for fairness
\cite{kamirancalders2012}, transplanted to imitation
learning. Whether the recovery is specific to the \emph{edit}, rather than an
artifact of upweighting any subset or of selecting fair data, is tested against
two controls (upweighting a size-matched random subset and upweighting the
corpus's fairest trajectories), with outcomes reported in
\S\ref{sec:experiments}.
