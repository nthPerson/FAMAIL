We evaluate FATE on real taxi GPS trajectories from two cities, Shenzhen and
San Francisco, by answering five questions.
\textbf{Q1}: does FATE reduce service disparity
(\S\ref{sec:exp-data-level})?
\textbf{Q2}: does it raise service in under-served regions, not just lower it
elsewhere (\S\ref{sec:exp-data-level})?
\textbf{Q3}: do the edited demonstrations improve downstream policies
(\S\ref{sec:exp-downstream})?
\textbf{Q4}: does FATE outperform existing and simpler baselines
(\S\ref{sec:exp-baselines})?
\textbf{Q5}: do the results hold across cities and demographic definitions
(\S\ref{sec:exp-robustness})?
Shenzhen answers Q1--Q4; San Francisco tests Q5.

\textbf{Protocol and statistics.} Every model-based comparison uses paired
seeds (identical seeding before each arm), so per-seed differences cancel
shared seed noise; sign counts are written $k/n$, the delta being positive on
$k$ of the $n$ seed pairs. At $n = 6$ we read effects from the mean $\Delta$,
its $t$-interval and dose-response monotonicity, and note that $0.031$ is the smallest
attainable $p$, reached when all six seed pairs agree in sign; the flagship comparison (the edited
demonstrations upweighted $30\times$, written w30; \S\ref{sec:exp-downstream})
is additionally run at $n = 12$ paired seeds in both cities. External metrics
are compared before versus after editing with no model in the loop, by a
paired bootstrap over the active grid units: each of $B = 1{,}000$ replicates
scores both corpora on the same resample. The resulting percentile intervals
understate true uncertainty: units are spatially correlated and demographics
district-constant (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\textbf{Metrics.} Fairness is measured on the service ratio $Y$ over active
grid units. Three instruments are external to the objective: \emph{disparate
impact} (DI, $\uparrow$), the \emph{demographic-parity gap} (DP, $\downarrow$;
on a continuous outcome DP is the supply/demand-ratio gap), and the
between-region \emph{Theil} index ($\downarrow$). We also report the two group
service \emph{levels} $\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ and
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{adv.})$, and the editor's own optimized
quantities $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$ and
$F_{\mathrm{fidelity}}$. These are the three metric classes referred to
below: optimized, design-targeted, and external. The
two grouping conventions (district extremes and median split) and the
instrument definitions are in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\textbf{Baseline algorithms.} No existing method edits trajectories for
fairness under a fidelity constraint, so we draw baselines from the two
nearest families: bounded perturbation methods, which edit trajectories but
not for fairness, and fairness interventions, which target fairness but act on
the model rather than on the demonstrations. The perturbation and oversampling arms
run at FATE's edit budget; the fairness interventions act on the raw corpus at
training time.
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=2pt,itemsep=0pt,parsep=0pt,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{ST-iFGSM} \cite{hu2023stifgsm}: iterative signed-gradient attack
on the frozen identity discriminator, with PGD-style random restart.
\item \textbf{FGSM} \cite{goodfellow2015fgsm}: the single full-budget step.
\item \textbf{Random jitter}: seeded $\varepsilon$-jitter, the direction
placebo for the two gradient arms.
\item \textbf{Demographic oversampling}: duplicates real trajectories
originating in disadvantaged regions, with an untargeted placebo that
fabricates the same volume without targeting.
\item \textbf{Instance reweighing} \cite{kamirancalders2012}: inverse
group-service weights on the raw corpus, weighting by demographic group rather
than by edit.
\item \textbf{Demographic-parity penalty} in the spirit of \cite{zheng2023}:
the parity gap added to the imitation loss at training time.
\item \textbf{BC- and GAN-generated corpora}: alternative sources of training
data, standing in for generate-instead-of-edit.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Edit-specific recovery under upweighting.} Upweighting the edited
demonstrations recovers the gain. It grows with the weight, and neither
control follows it (Fig.~\ref{fig:dose}). The most-fair control reaches
$+0.0033$ at w10, under a sixth of the edited arm's gain, then fades to n.s. At the adopted dose the flagship
comparison runs at $n = 12$ paired seeds: $+0.0297 \pm 0.0029$ (w30), 12/12
positive, exact Wilcoxon $p = .00049$, with both controls null at the same
$n = 12$ (random $-0.0017$, most-fair $+0.0012$, n.s.). The gain saturates,
placing w30 at the knee rather than at a tuned endpoint. Fidelity-A is
unchanged at every weight, and Fidelity-B rises with the dose: the expected
signature of shifting population statistics toward the edited slice. Per-dose
values, $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$ transfer, and model-level variance are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:results}.
