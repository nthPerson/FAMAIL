\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}
\textbf{Datasets.} The primary city is Shenzhen: a 50-driver sample of real
taxi GPS trajectories on a $48{\times}90$ grid of $0.01^{\circ}$ cells, with
district-level demographics. Raw data preprocessing included removing 106{,}677
pickups with almost no matching drop-offs --- stuck GPS, not real demand.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (grid, cleanup counts)
The external-validity city is San Francisco (Cabspotting): a 12-driver
subsample matched on fleet density (\texttt{sf12}, $32{\times}30$ grid), with
the same demographic feature names filled from ACS tract values. Density matching is
required --- the full 536-taxi fleet saturates the supply measure and leaves
no editable fairness gradient.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (sf12 selection, density matching)


\textbf{Editor configuration.} All edited corpora use the trim+lift editor of
\S\ref{sec:editor}, with $\alpha = (0.1, 0.8, 0.1)$ weights selected using the 
three-class criterion of \S\ref{sec:exp-robustness}, $\varepsilon = 2$, and the 
skip-on-infeasible rule of \S\ref{sec:phys-validity}. Shenzhen: 
$k = 10{,}000$ (2{,}337 trim + 7{,}545 lift after filtering); San Francisco: 
$k = 2{,}000$ (1{,}330 trim + 629 lift).
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-10T02-06-37_alpha_sweep_s10_c80_f10_filtered/metrics.json (SZ split)
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-11T11-31-55_supply_lift_a10_sf12_filtered/metrics.json (SF split)
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/weight-sensitivity/DECISION.md + EXTENDED_FRONTIER.md (decision 2026-07-11)

\textbf{Protocol and statistics.} Every model-based comparison uses paired
seeds (identical seeding before each arm), so per-seed differences cancel
shared seed noise. With $n = 6$ paired seeds we read effects from the mean
$\Delta$, its $t$-interval, and dose-response monotonicity, and treat
$p = 0.031$ only as a sign-unanimity certificate. The flagship w30 comparison
is additionally run at $n = 12$ paired seeds in both cities, where an exact
Wilcoxon $p$ can survive multiple-comparison correction.
External fairness metrics are compared before versus after editing on the data alone,
with no model in the loop: one bootstrap resample of the active grid units
is applied to both sides ($B = 1{,}000$, percentile), so the interval
isolates the edit. Units are spatially correlated and demographics are
district-constant, so these intervals understate true uncertainty. 
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} gives the exact-Wilcoxon floor arithmetic and
why no driver-level bootstrap is available.
% src: PAPER/argument/04_evaluation.md §3; PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §1, §5

\textbf{Metrics and claim discipline.} We separate three metric classes and
label every claim as follows: (i) \emph{optimized} --- $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$,
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, $F_{\mathrm{fidelity}}$, the quantities the editor's
gradient sees; (ii) \emph{design-targeted but not optimized} --- the
supply\slash demand ratios and $\mathrm{mean}(Y)$ per group that lift was
aimed at, so
improvement there is confirmatory; and (iii) \emph{genuinely external} ---
demographic parity, disparate impact, the Theil index, and the distinct-taxi
supply recount, absent from the objective.
``Improves metrics it never optimizes'' claims apply to class (iii) only. The
external instruments are computed on the service ratio $Y$ over active units,
grouped per equity axis: \emph{demographic parity} (DP; on a continuous outcome
DP \emph{is} the supply/demand-ratio gap --- one number, reported once),
\emph{disparate impact} (DI), and the between-region \emph{Theil} index,
alongside the two group \emph{levels} $\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$
and $\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{adv.})$, which are class (ii) context, not
external instruments. The strict count of distinct external instruments is
therefore \{DI, DP/gap, Theil\}.
Grouping conventions and instrument definitions are in
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.

\textbf{The lift-up claim rides the supply channel.} The
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ rise is a
\emph{design-targeted} (class (ii)) quantity --- the lift mechanism was aimed at it --- so
we decompose it rather than celebrate it. Evaluating $Y$ at
$(S,D) \to (S,D') \to (S',D')$ splits the delta exactly into a demand move and
a supply move ($B{=}2{,}000$). The demand channel (pickup relocation) carries
large variance and is not significant, $+0.0352$ [$-0.0095$, $+0.0814$]. The
supply channel (rerouted tails adding presence) is significant under
\emph{both} accounting conventions. Under the optimizer's
\emph{fractional-presence} convention it is $+0.0176$ [$+0.0128$, $+0.0225$]; % lint-allow: coincidental CI bound (s10 fractional-presence/tier-1 interval, not the trim-only headline delta)
under the \emph{distinct-taxi} accounting it is $+0.0411$ [$+0.0346$,
$+0.0479$]. That accounting re-counts supply from raw GPS as distinct taxis
per neighborhood-hour with the edited tails substituted, and it reproduces
the production supply grid exactly (MAE 0.0, 100\% history match): the
stricter semantics, not an approximation.

\subsection{Downstream Propagation (Shenzhen)}\label{sec:exp-downstream}

\textbf{Data quality (four sources).} We score four corpora --- raw, edited,
BC-generated, and GAN-generated --- on fairness and both fidelity axes, with
generators trained from scratch for this comparison and Fidelity-A gated using 
the realism guardrail of \S\ref{sec:objective}; the claim under test is
that the edited corpus is the fairest source that remains faithful on both
realism axes. The claim holds: the edited corpus is the fairest source
faithful to driver identity (Fidelity-A gate passed, matched 0.848 vs.\ mismatched
0.193), with Fidelity-B 0.187 the disclosed divergence of the bounded edits
(Table~\ref{tab:l1}, Appendix~\ref{app:results}). One instability we surface
rather than smooth: the Shenzhen GAN's Fidelity-B is bimodal across seeds, 
while the SF GAN stays uniformly healthy (\S\ref{sec:exp-sf}); the
per-seed spread is in Appendix~\ref{app:results}.

\textbf{Vanilla training averages the edit away.} A driver-conditioned BC
policy trained on the edited corpus at uniform weight is not fairer than one
trained on raw: paired $\Delta F_{\mathrm{demo}} = +0.0016$ ($n = 12$,
7/12 positive, $p = 0.11$, n.s.) --- the null predicted in 
\S\ref{sec:downstream}, and the reason the upweighting recipe exists.

\subsection{Data-Augmentation Baselines}\label{sec:exp-baselines}
None of the baselines below optimizes a fairness objective; they are
\emph{editing-quality} baselines that test whether FATE's gains come from
its objective or merely from bounded perturbation (or corpus resampling)
per se. Fairness is accordingly \emph{expected not to improve} under the
perturbation arms.
% src: PAPER/baselines/README.md (Meeting-41 framing)

\textbf{Perturbation arms.} Three arms attack the same trajectories the
headline edit selected, at the same $\varepsilon = 2$: iterative and
single-step signed-gradient attacks on the frozen identity discriminator
\cite{hu2023stifgsm}, and
a seeded random-jitter placebo. The gradient arms are \emph{iFGSM\slash
FGSM with random restart} \cite{kurakin2017ifgsm,goodfellow2015fgsm}
(PGD-style random initialization within the
$\varepsilon$ ball \cite{madry2018pgd}); a pre-registered $\delta = 0$ no-op ablation did not
stall because the
deployed head compares embeddings by \emph{concatenation}, not stationary at
an identical pair (Appendix~\ref{app:editor}). The random-start arms are the
reported ones. Results: Table~\ref{tab:baselines}.

One result lands against the pre-registered expectation: random jitter
\emph{does} raise $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, by $+0.0135$ --- indiscriminately
diffusing the selected trajectories diffuses their over-service too --- but it
does so by breaking the trajectories: 98.8\% of its perturbed trajectories
violate king-move adjacency and its distributional divergence reaches 0.447,
against the edited corpus's disclosed 0.187, while FATE edits are
king-compliant by construction (per-arm figures in
Appendix~\ref{app:results}). Identity fidelity survives every arm
($\approx 0.846$) --- too small a perturbation to break driver identity, so
adjacency and distributional axes carry the comparison. The three perturbation arms replicate on San
Francisco --- all well below the editor's $+0.0316$, all violating adjacency
wholesale --- and the random-jitter fairness surprise does not recur there;
random is the weakest mover.
% src: famail_temporal/baselines/baseline_table/baseline_table.json (random Fid-B(JS)/adjacency; Fid-A ~0.846)

\textbf{Demographic oversampling.} The naive lift-up baseline: duplicate
real trajectories originating in demographically disadvantaged regions
\cite{kamirancalders2012,zietlow2022} (all
three equity axes, phantom driver IDs, rigid $\pm 1$-cell jitter), additively
rebuilding both demand and supply at doses matched to FATE's budget. At
the matched duplication dose $d = 10{,}000$, targeted oversampling improves
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ by $+0.0153$ (dose-monotone:
$+0.0059$, $+0.0097$, $+0.0153$) --- but it fabricates 10.5\% of the corpus
to get there, where FATE redistributes observed behavior at zero inflation
and reaches $+0.0226$. The untargeted placebo (same fabrication without targeting)
\emph{degrades} fairness, $-0.0172$, so the gain is targeting-specific; and
the placebo's DP gap explodes ($+2.8$) because fabricated supply lands
predominantly in already-advantaged cells --- a measured demonstration that
ratio metrics can be gamed by fabrication, the demand-endo\-ge\-ne\-ity concern of
\S\ref{sec:leveling} made concrete. Two disclosures: the corpus cannot even
supply the matched dose without re-duplication (Appendix~\ref{app:results}),
and fidelity is not scored for this arm --- duplicates of real trajectories
pass any realism check by construction.
% src: PAPER/baselines/demographic-oversampling/FINDINGS.md §2-5

\textbf{Fairness-method baselines (training-side).} The remaining comparison
is against fairness methods proper, applied where they usually live: at
training time, on the raw corpus, scored by the full paired protocol.
Instance reweighing in the Kamiran--Calders style
\cite{kamirancalders2012} --- inverse group-service weights favoring
disadvantaged-origin demonstrations --- moves demand-adjusted fairness the
\emph{wrong} way ($-0.0227$, all six seeds negative, $p = .031$) while
paying the same distributional cost as w30 upweighting (Fidelity-B
$+0.0144$), and its rolled-out policies still tilt allocation away from the % lint-allow: coincidental value (fair_reweigh paired Fid-B delta, not the trim-only F_demo headline)
disadvantaged group ($-0.0010$, 0/6, $p = .031$). An in-processing penalty
in the spirit of \cite{zheng2023} --- the imitation loss plus $\lambda$
times the demo\-graph\-ic-parity gap in predicted next-cell mass --- is inert at
every trainable dose, destructive only where it dominates. The signed
formulation (advantaged minus disadvantaged, the direction the data
over-serves) and the absolute-value formulation behave the same at every
$\lambda$; neither offers a constructive operating range. The full
$\lambda$ grid, both formulations, the collapse magnitudes, and the
$10^{-5}$ loss-share derivation are in Appendix~\ref{app:results}.
Neither training-side intervention reproduces any part of the recovery
that editing the demonstrations provides.

\subsection{Robustness and Sensitivity}\label{sec:exp-robustness}

The control rows are re-measured deliberately: the random placebo is
null-to-negative under every feature set, and the most-fair selection
control --- null on the primary set --- is significantly \emph{positive}
under both alternate sets ($+0.0054$ and $+0.0072$ at w30, a fifth to a
quarter of the edited arm's gain). We report the strongest controls we
found rather than average them away: even where selection captures a
minority share, the edited arm's gain is at least $3\times$ larger at
every dose, which is the edit-specificity claim under its hardest test.
Vanilla transfer likewise stays an order of magnitude below the upweighted
gain under every set, reaching sign-unanimity only under the
housing--GDP--compensation set ($+0.0022$, a tenth of its w30 gain).

\textbf{Weight sensitivity.} A weight sweep of six full editing runs, 
each scored on all three metric classes, shows the optimized
$\Delta F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ flat --- within $0.001$ --- for
$\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}} \in [0, 0.55]$, while the class (ii) supply-channel
lift-up declines monotonically with $\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}}$ and is significant
under both accounting conventions only for $\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}} \le 0.2$.
The adopted
$(0.1, 0.8, 0.1)$ is the three-class criterion's selection: any weighting in
the flat range delivers the same $\Delta F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, and the low
$\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}}$ is what keeps the supply-channel lift-up
significant. The full sweep and
figure are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:results}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha-pareto}).

\textbf{Feature-set robustness.} $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ is specific to its
demographic feature set, and its absolute magnitude shifts under each 
configuration, so every layer of the argument is re-measured under
two alternate sets (Table~\ref{tab:featsets}, Appendix~\ref{app:results}). The
design-targeted lift-up reproduces: the total
$\Delta\,\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ carries a bootstrap interval
excluding zero on all three sets, and the supply channel is individually
