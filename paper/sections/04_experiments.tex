\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} The primary city is Shenzhen: a 50-driver sample of real taxi
GPS trajectories on a $48{\times}90$ grid of $0.01^{\circ}$ cells, with
district-level demographics. Raw data contained per-driver ``stuck-GPS'' pickup
sinks (single plates emitting thousands of phantom pickups); a signature rule
removed 106{,}677 phantom pickups before any experiment.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (grid, cleanup counts)
The external-validity city is San Francisco (Cabspotting): a 12-driver,
fleet-density-matched subsample (\texttt{sf12}, $32{\times}30$ grid) with the
same demographic feature names filled from ACS tract values. Density matching
is required --- the full 536-taxi fleet saturates the supply measure and leaves
no editable fairness gradient.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (sf12 selection, density matching)

\textbf{Editor configuration.} All edited corpora use the trim+lift editor of
\S\ref{sec:editor} with $\alpha = (0.1, 0.8, 0.1)$ --- the configuration the
three-ring selection criterion picks on the measured frontier
(\S\ref{sec:exp-robustness}) --- $\varepsilon = 2$, and the
skip-on-infeasible rule. Shenzhen: $k = 10{,}000$ (2{,}337 trim + 7{,}545
lift after filtering); San Francisco: $k = 2{,}000$ (1{,}330 trim + 629 lift).
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-10T02-06-37_alpha_sweep_s10_c80_f10_filtered/metrics.json (SZ split)
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-11T11-31-55_supply_lift_a10_sf12_filtered/metrics.json (SF split)
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/weight-sensitivity/DECISION.md + EXTENDED_FRONTIER.md (decision 2026-07-11)

\textbf{Protocol and statistics.} Every model-based comparison uses paired
seeds (identical seeding before each arm), so per-seed differences cancel
shared seed noise. With $n = 6$ paired seeds the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank
$p$ floors at $0.03125$, attained exactly when all six seeds share a sign; we
therefore read effects from the mean $\Delta$, its $t$-interval, and
dose-response monotonicity, and treat $p = 0.031$ only as a sign-unanimity
certificate. Suites with $n = 5$ seeds cannot reach $p < 0.05$ (floor 0.0625)
and are reported as effect-versus-noise. Data-level before/after deltas on
grid metrics carry paired unit-level bootstrap intervals ($B = 1{,}000$,
percentile); units are spatially correlated and demographics are
district-constant, so these intervals are first-order.
% src: PAPER/argument/04_evaluation.md §3; PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §1, §5

\textbf{Metric firewall.} We separate three rings of metrics and label every
claim accordingly: (i) \emph{optimized} --- $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$,
$F_{\mathrm{causal}}$, $F_{\mathrm{fidelity}}$, the quantities the editor's
gradient sees; (ii) \emph{design-targeted but not optimized} --- the
supply/demand ratio family ($\mathrm{mean}(Y)$ by group), which the lift
mechanism was aimed at, so improvement there is confirmatory; and (iii)
\emph{genuinely external} --- demographic parity, disparate impact, the Theil
index, per-group service levels, and the distinct-taxi supply recount, none of
which appear in the objective. ``Improves metrics it never optimizes'' claims
ride ring (iii) only.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §13

\textbf{External fairness instruments.} On the service ratio $Y$ over active
units, grouped per equity axis (housing price, compensation, migrant share; two
grouping conventions --- district extremes and median split): the two group
\emph{levels} $\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadvantaged})$ and
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{advantaged})$; \emph{demographic parity} (DP), the
signed gap between them (on a continuous outcome DP \emph{is} the
supply/demand-ratio gap --- one number, reported once); \emph{disparate impact}
(DI), their ratio (1 = parity); and the between-region \emph{Theil} index
(grouping-independent). The honest count of distinct external instruments is
therefore \{DI, DP/gap, Theil\} plus the descriptive levels.
% src: PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §1, §4.3 (DP≡gap disclosure)

\textbf{The lift-up claim rides the supply channel.} The
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ rise is a ring-(ii)
\emph{design-targeted} quantity --- the lift mechanism was aimed at it --- so
we decompose it rather than celebrate it. Evaluating $Y$ at
$(S,D) \to (S,D') \to (S',D')$ splits the delta exactly into a demand move and
a supply move (Table~\ref{tab:channels}). The demand channel (pickup
relocation) carries large variance and is not significant; the supply channel
(rerouted tails adding presence) is significant under \emph{both} accounting
conventions --- tier-1 (the optimizer's fractional-presence convention) and
tier-2, which re-counts supply from raw GPS as distinct taxis per
neighborhood-hour with the edited tails substituted. The recount machinery
reproduces the production supply grid exactly (MAE 0.0) with 100\% history
matching, so tier-2 is the honest semantics, not an approximation.

\subsection{Ablation: Trim-Only versus Trim+Lift}\label{sec:exp-ablation}

(\S\ref{sec:editor}), running the editor with the lift phase disabled
reproduces the demand-only editor exactly, and every difference in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} is attributable to the lift mechanism. The ablation
certifies the design claim of \S\ref{sec:leveling}: a demand-only editor
improves fairness metrics while leaving the under-served group's absolute
service untouched --- leveling down --- and the supply channel is what changes
that.

\subsection{Data-Augmentation Baselines}\label{sec:exp-baselines}

None of the baselines below optimizes a fairness objective; they are
\emph{editing-quality} baselines that test whether FAMAIL's gains come from
its objective or merely from bounded perturbation (or corpus resampling)
per se. Fairness is accordingly \emph{expected not to improve} under the
perturbation arms.
% src: PAPER/baselines/README.md (Meeting-41 framing)

\textbf{Perturbation arms.} Three arms attack the same trajectories the
headline edit selected, at the same $\varepsilon = 2$: iterative and
single-step signed-gradient attacks on the frozen identity discriminator, and
a seeded random-jitter placebo. A naming note required for correctness: the
gradient arms are \emph{iFGSM/FGSM with random restart} --- the frozen Siamese
identity head has zero subgradient at an identical pair, so a textbook attack
initialized at $\delta = 0$ is a provable no-op; we retain the $\delta = 0$
initialization as an ablation row to demonstrate exactly that.
% src: famail_temporal/baselines/STATUS.md (paper-facing notes: random-restart naming, vanilla no-op)
Results: Table~\ref{tab:baselines}.
% TODO(run:Q1 -> famail_temporal/baselines/baseline_table/ + PAPER/baselines/comparison/) perturbation-arm rows pending GPU stage q1

\textbf{Demographic oversampling.} The naive lifting-up baseline: duplicate
real trajectories originating in demographically disadvantaged regions (all
three equity axes, phantom driver IDs, rigid $\pm 1$-cell jitter), rebuilding
demand \emph{and} supply additively, at doses matched to FAMAIL's budget. At
the matched dose $d = 10{,}000$, targeted oversampling improves
$F_{\mathrm{causal}}$ by $+0.0153$ (dose-monotone:
$+0.0059 \to +0.0097 \to +0.0153$) --- but it fabricates 10.5\% of the corpus
to get there, where FAMAIL redistributes observed behavior at zero inflation
and reaches $+0.0226$. The untargeted placebo (same fabrication, no targeting)
\emph{degrades} fairness ($-0.0172$), so the gain is targeting-specific; and
the placebo's DP gap explodes ($+2.8$) because fabricated supply lands
predominantly in already-advantaged cells --- a measured demonstration that
ratio metrics can be gamed by fabrication, the demand-endogeneity concern of
\S\ref{sec:leveling} made concrete. Two disclosures: the corpus cannot even
supply the matched dose honestly (only 8{,}241 distinct disadvantaged-origin
trajectories exist, so 1{,}759 draws are re-duplications), and fidelity is not
scored for this arm --- duplicates of real trajectories pass any realism check
by construction.

\subsection{External Validity: San Francisco}\label{sec:exp-sf}

The entire protocol reruns on San Francisco with no algorithm, metric, or
fidelity change; because $F_{\mathrm{causal}}$ is city-specific and
associational (ACS proxies, not hukou), San Francisco tests whether the
\emph{conclusions} reproduce, never whether magnitudes match.

\textbf{Data level.} $F_{\mathrm{causal}}$ rises 0.8752 $\to$ 0.9067
($+0.0316$), $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$ $+0.0139$, with Fidelity-A stability and % lint-allow: coincidental value (SF a10 F_spatial delta, not the trim-only F_causal headline)
lift's Fidelity-B cost re-measured on this corpus by the downstream suite
(pending below). Every external instrument improves: Theil $-0.0079$
(CI $[-0.0094, -0.0065]$), and the migrant axis is significant under
\emph{both} grouping conventions on the DP gap (district extremes $-0.0729$,
CI $[-0.1132, -0.0259]$; median split $-0.0370$, CI $[-0.0733, -0.0002]$),
with DI significant under district extremes ($+0.0058$,
CI $[+0.0004, +0.0103]$) and n.s.\ under median split --- a grouping
dependence we state rather than hide (the SF sample is 12 drivers).
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/data/a10/sf12_a10_metrics.json + sf12_a10_external_fairness.json

\textbf{The supply channel replicates; the ratio does not.} The supply
channel is again positive and significant ($+0.0209$,
CI $[+0.0122, +0.0300]$) --- tier-1 accounting only, a \emph{lower bound} on
Shenzhen's evidence (the distinct-taxi tier-2 recount is not plumbed for the
SF pipeline). But as on the prior corpus, lift also reroutes \emph{pickups}
into under-served cells, raising recorded demand there: the demand channel is
significantly negative ($-0.0533$, CI $[-0.0798, -0.0298]$) and the total
