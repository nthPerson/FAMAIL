\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Datasets.} The primary city is Shenzhen: a 50-driver sample of real taxi
GPS trajectories on a $48{\times}90$ grid of $0.01^{\circ}$ cells, with
district-level demographics. Raw data contained per-driver ``stuck-GPS'' pickup
sinks (single plates emitting thousands of phantom pickups); a signature rule
removed 106{,}677 phantom pickups before any experiment.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (grid, cleanup counts)
The external-validity city is San Francisco (Cabspotting): a 12-driver,
fleet-density-matched subsample (\texttt{sf12}, $32{\times}30$ grid) with the
same demographic feature names filled from ACS tract values. Density matching
is required --- the full 536-taxi fleet saturates the supply measure and leaves
no editable fairness gradient.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (sf12 selection, density matching)

\textbf{Editor configuration.} All edited corpora use the trim+lift editor of
\S\ref{sec:editor} with $\alpha = (0.1, 0.8, 0.1)$ --- the configuration the
three-class criterion picks on the measured frontier
(\S\ref{sec:exp-robustness}) --- $\varepsilon = 2$, and the
skip-on-infeasible rule. Shenzhen: $k = 10{,}000$ (2{,}337 trim + 7{,}545
lift after filtering); San Francisco: $k = 2{,}000$ (1{,}330 trim + 629 lift).
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-10T02-06-37_alpha_sweep_s10_c80_f10_filtered/metrics.json (SZ split)
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-11T11-31-55_supply_lift_a10_sf12_filtered/metrics.json (SF split)
% src: PAPER/objective-motivation/weight-sensitivity/DECISION.md + EXTENDED_FRONTIER.md (decision 2026-07-11)

\textbf{Protocol and statistics.} Every model-based comparison uses paired
seeds (identical seeding before each arm), so per-seed differences cancel
shared seed noise. With $n = 6$ paired seeds the two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank
$p$ floors at $0.03125$, attained exactly when all six seeds share a sign; we
therefore read effects from the mean $\Delta$, its $t$-interval, and
dose-response monotonicity, and treat $p = 0.031$ only as a sign-unanimity
certificate. The flagship w30 comparison is additionally run at $n = 12$
paired seeds in both cities (floor $.00049$), where an exact Wilcoxon $p$
can survive multiple-comparison correction. Suites with $n = 5$ seeds cannot reach $p < 0.05$ (floor 0.0625)
and are reported as effect-versus-noise. Data-level before/after deltas on
grid metrics carry paired unit-level bootstrap intervals ($B = 1{,}000$,
percentile); units are spatially correlated and demographics are
district-constant, so these intervals are first-order.
% src: PAPER/argument/04_evaluation.md §3; PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §1, §5

\textbf{Metric classes and claim discipline.} We separate three metric classes and label every
claim accordingly: (i) \emph{optimized} --- $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$,
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, $F_{\mathrm{fidelity}}$, the quantities the editor's
gradient sees; (ii) \emph{design-targeted but not optimized} --- the
family of supply/demand ratios, $\mathrm{mean}(Y)$ by group, which the lift
mechanism was aimed at, so improvement there is confirmatory; and (iii)
\emph{genuinely external} --- demographic parity, disparate impact, the Theil
index, per-group service levels, and the distinct-taxi supply recount, none of
which appear in the objective. ``Improves metrics it never optimizes'' claims
ride class (iii) only.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §13

\textbf{External fairness instruments.} On the service ratio $Y$ over active
units, grouped per equity axis (housing price, compensation, migrant share; two
grouping conventions --- district extremes and median split): the two group
\emph{levels} $\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadvantaged})$ and
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{advantaged})$; \emph{demographic parity} (DP), the
signed gap between them (on a continuous outcome DP \emph{is} the
supply/demand-ratio gap --- one number, reported once); \emph{disparate impact}
(DI), their ratio (1 = parity); and the between-region \emph{Theil} index
(grouping-independent). The strict count of distinct external instruments is
therefore \{DI, DP/gap, Theil\} plus the descriptive levels.
% src: PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §1, §4.3 (DP≡gap disclosure)

\textbf{The lift-up claim rides the supply channel.} The
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ rise is a
\emph{design-targeted} (class (ii)) quantity --- the lift mechanism was aimed at it --- so
we decompose it rather than celebrate it. Evaluating $Y$ at
$(S,D) \to (S,D') \to (S',D')$ splits the delta exactly into a demand move and
a supply move (Table~\ref{tab:channels}). The demand channel (pickup
relocation) carries large variance and is not significant; the supply channel
(rerouted tails adding presence) is significant under \emph{both} accounting
conventions --- tier-1 (the optimizer's fractional-presence convention) and
tier-2, which re-counts supply from raw GPS as distinct taxis per
neighborhood-hour with the edited tails substituted. The recount machinery
reproduces the production supply grid exactly (MAE 0.0) with 100\% history
matching, so tier-2 is the stricter semantics, not an approximation.
% src: <s10>/channel_decomposition.json + <s10>/supply_recount.json
% src (recount validity properties): PAPER/supply-lift/FINDINGS.md §4 (G2, machinery unchanged)

\textbf{Realism.} Fidelity-A stability and lift's expected Fidelity-B
distributional cost (whole-tail relocation shifts trajectory-shape statistics
more than a pickup-only move) are re-measured on the adopted corpus:
Fidelity-A 0.844 vs.\ raw 0.848; corpus-level Fidelity-B 0.187, concentrated
in exactly the components a rerouted tail must move --- terminal cell
(0.635), net displacement (0.147), coverage (0.142) --- while trajectory
length, which neither editing phase alters, barely registers (0.060) --- a
by-design trade-off we disclose rather than net out.
% src: famail_temporal/results/level1_table_v2/supply_lift_shz_5seed/level1_v2_multiseed.json (edited row + fidelity_b_per_component)

\textbf{Provenance disclosures.} About 5\% of trim edits (118/2{,}455) had no
king-compliant repair and were reverted post hoc; the rule was adopted before
its metric effects were computed, and it moved every metric favorably --- the
infeasible edits were actively counterproductive. The pre-build oracle gate
bounded the supply-channel headroom at $+0.786$ on
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ --- $2.6\times$ the pre-registered
go/no-go threshold of $+0.3$ (full accounting $+0.882$); the realized $+0.053$
sits far below the unconstrained ceiling, as a realism-bounded editor should.

\subsection{Ablation: Trim-Only versus Trim+Lift}\label{sec:exp-ablation}

Because trim's optimization is frozen inside the combined editor
(\S\ref{sec:editor}), running the editor with the lift phase disabled
reproduces the demand-only editor exactly, and every difference in
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} is attributable to the lift mechanism. The ablation
certifies the design claim of \S\ref{sec:leveling}: a demand-only editor
improves fairness metrics while leaving the under-served group's absolute
service untouched --- the gap closes only from the top --- and the supply
channel is what changes that.

Three rows carry the qualitative story, and the structural expectation from
\S\ref{sec:leveling} re-verifies exactly at the adopted weights: under
trim-only, every external instrument still improves significantly (DP
$-0.611$, DI $+0.0098$, Theil $-0.0059$, all CIs excluding zero) --- but the
disadvantaged group's service level is unchanged to four decimals
($7.0734 \to 7.0734$) while the advantaged level falls; the improvement is
pure top-down redistribution: the under-served level does not move. Under
trim+lift the same level rises with a bootstrap
interval excluding zero.
% src: famail_temporal/baselines/external_fairness/results/shenzhen-trimonly-a10/external_fairness.json
The secondary spatial metric flips from a small cost to a gain.
Per Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, the lift mechanism is not an incremental tweak
to the demand-only editor but the difference between redistributing service
and providing it.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/FINDINGS.md §3 (the key qualitative change paragraph)

\subsection{Data-Augmentation Baselines}\label{sec:exp-baselines}

None of the baselines below optimizes a fairness objective; they are
\emph{editing-quality} baselines that test whether FATE's gains come from
its objective or merely from bounded perturbation (or corpus resampling)
per se. Fairness is accordingly \emph{expected not to improve} under the
perturbation arms.
% src: PAPER/baselines/README.md (Meeting-41 framing)

\textbf{Perturbation arms.} Three arms attack the same trajectories the
headline edit selected, at the same $\varepsilon = 2$: iterative and
single-step signed-gradient attacks on the frozen identity discriminator, and
a seeded random-jitter placebo. A naming note: the gradient arms are
\emph{iFGSM/FGSM with random restart} (PGD-style random initialization within
the $\varepsilon$ ball). We had pre-registered $\delta = 0$-initialized
ablation arms expecting a no-op --- a difference-mode Siamese head has zero
subgradient at an identical pair --- but the deployed discriminator compares
embeddings by \emph{concatenation}, which is not stationary there: measured,
the $\delta = 0$ arms attack rather than stall (the iterative arm lands
within $0.0003$ of its random-start twin; the single-step arm differs with
initialization, as a one-step method must). The stationarity assumption held
only for the head we did not deploy, and the random-start arms are the
reported ones.
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-13T06-26-19/…06-27-50 arm metrics (nors); deployed checkpoint combination_mode="concatenation" (fidelity/model.py); gradient-at-zero verified live 2026-07-13
Results: Table~\ref{tab:baselines}.

The arms behave as fidelity baselines. One result lands against the
pre-registered expectation: random jitter \emph{does} raise
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$, by $+0.0135$ --- indiscriminately diffusing the selected
trajectories diffuses their over-service too --- but it does so by breaking
the trajectories: 98.8\% of its perturbed trajectories violate king-move
adjacency and its distributional divergence reaches 0.447, against the
edited corpus's disclosed 0.187 (iFGSM: 54.4\%, 0.143; FGSM: 91.4\%,
0.339; FATE edits are king-compliant by construction). Identity fidelity
survives every arm ($\approx 0.846$, gate passed) --- at $\varepsilon = 2$
the perturbations are too small to break driver identity, which is exactly
why identity fidelity alone cannot rank editing quality here and the
adjacency and distributional axes carry the comparison. The three
perturbation arms replicate on San Francisco: every arm lands well below the
editor ($+0.0122$ iFGSM, $+0.0176$ FGSM, $+0.0049$ random, against the
editor's $+0.0316$) while violating adjacency wholesale
(72.0/87.0/97.5\%) and paying $2$--$4\times$ the edited corpus's
distributional divergence (0.198--0.418 against 0.098); the random-jitter
fairness surprise does not recur there --- random is the weakest mover.

% src: famail_temporal/baselines/baseline_table/baseline_table.json (Fid-A/Fid-B(JS)/adjacency-violation columns)

\textbf{Demographic oversampling.} The naive lift-up baseline: duplicate
real trajectories originating in demographically disadvantaged regions (all
three equity axes, phantom driver IDs, rigid $\pm 1$-cell jitter), additively
rebuilding both demand and supply at doses matched to FATE's budget. At
the matched duplication dose $d = 10{,}000$, targeted oversampling improves
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ by $+0.0153$ (dose-monotone:
$+0.0059$, $+0.0097$, $+0.0153$) --- but it fabricates 10.5\% of the corpus
to get there, where FATE redistributes observed behavior at zero inflation
and reaches $+0.0226$. The untargeted placebo (same fabrication without targeting)
\emph{degrades} fairness, $-0.0172$, so the gain is targeting-specific; and
the placebo's DP gap explodes ($+2.8$) because fabricated supply lands
predominantly in already-advantaged cells --- a measured demonstration that
ratio metrics can be gamed by fabrication, the demand-endogeneity concern of
\S\ref{sec:leveling} made concrete. Two disclosures: the corpus cannot even
supply the matched dose without re-duplication (only 8{,}241 distinct disadvantaged-origin
trajectories exist, so 1{,}759 draws repeat earlier ones), and fidelity is not
scored for this arm --- duplicates of real trajectories pass any realism check
by construction.
% src: PAPER/baselines/demographic-oversampling/FINDINGS.md §2-5

\textbf{Fairness-method baselines (training-side).} The remaining comparison
is against fairness methods proper, applied where they usually live: at
training time, on the raw corpus, scored by the full paired protocol.
Instance reweighing in the Kamiran--Calders style
\cite{kamirancalders2012} --- inverse group-service weights favoring
disadvantaged-origin demonstrations --- moves demand-adjusted fairness the
\emph{wrong} way ($-0.0227$, all six seeds negative, $p = .031$) while
paying the same distributional cost as w30 upweighting (Fidelity-B
$+0.0144$), and its rolled-out policies still tilt allocation away from the % lint-allow: coincidental value (fair_reweigh paired Fid-B delta, not the trim-only F_demo headline)
disadvantaged group ($-0.0010$, 0/6, $p = .031$). An in-processing penalty
in the spirit of \cite{zheng2023} --- the imitation loss plus $\lambda$
times the demographic-parity gap in predicted next-cell mass, tested in
both the signed formulation (advantaged minus disadvantaged, the direction
the data over-serves) and the absolute-value formulation --- is inert at
every dose that trains stably (six-seed suites:
$|\Delta F_{\mathrm{demo}}| \le 0.0009$ signed at
$\lambda \in \{1,$ $3.16,$ $10\}$, $+0.0008$ absolute at $\lambda = 10$,
all n.s.; single-seed probes at $\lambda = 100$ agree; the term contributes
on the order of $10^{-5}$ of the loss) and destructive at the dose where it
finally dominates: at $\lambda = 1000$ the signed form collapses
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ by $-0.2053$ with Fidelity-B at 0.514, the absolute
form by $-0.1293$ (6/6 seeds) with Fidelity-B at 0.363, against raw's
$\approx 0.013$. The shallower absolute collapse is the gap pushing back
once it crosses zero; neither formulation offers a constructive operating
range.

\subsection{Robustness and Sensitivity}\label{sec:exp-robustness}

The control rows are re-measured deliberately: the random placebo is
null-to-negative under every feature set, and the most-fair selection
control --- null on the primary set --- is significantly \emph{positive}
under both alternate sets ($+0.0054$ and $+0.0072$ at w30, a fifth to a
quarter of the edited arm's gain). We report the strongest controls we
found rather than average them away: even where selection captures a
minority share, the edited arm's gain is at least $3\times$ larger at
every dose, which is the edit-specificity claim under its hardest test.
Vanilla transfer likewise stays an order of magnitude below the upweighted
gain under every set, reaching sign-unanimity only under the
housing--GDP--compensation set ($+0.0022$, a tenth of its w30 gain).
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/data/a10/shz_4feat_a10_weighted_bc_paired_stats.json (most_fair arms +0.0059/+0.0059/+0.0072 all p=.031; edited/most_fair min ratio 3.05 at w10)

\textbf{Weight sensitivity.} Fig.~\ref{fig:alpha-pareto} reports the weight
sweep: six full editing runs across the simplex, each scored on all three
metric classes. The optimized $\Delta F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ is flat --- within
$0.001$ --- for $\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}} \in [0, 0.55]$, while the class (ii)
supply-channel lift-up declines monotonically with $\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}}$ ---
significant on both tiers only for $\alpha_{\mathrm{sp}} \le 0.2$ --- and
class (iii) instruments improve significantly at every point. The adopted
$(0.1, 0.8, 0.1)$ is the three-class criterion's selection; the sweep shows
the choice is robust on the primary axis and load-bearing only for the
lift-up property.

\textbf{Feature-set robustness.} $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ is specific to its
demographic feature set, so every layer of the argument is re-measured
under two alternate sets (Table~\ref{tab:featsets}). The claim is
directional reproduction, not magnitude equality --- absolute
$F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ scales shift with the feature set by construction.
The design-targeted lift-up also reproduces: the total
$\Delta\,\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ carries a bootstrap
interval excluding zero on all three sets, and the supply channel is
individually significant on all three --- under \emph{both} accounting
conventions (distinct-taxi tier-2: $+0.0411$, $+0.0211$, $+0.0771$, every
interval excluding zero). The channel mix is not constant ---
on the primary set the demand channel is n.s., while under both alternate
sets it is significant and carries most of the larger totals --- so the
added-presence mechanism replicates while the demand-side share varies with
the feature set, and we report the decomposition rather than the total
alone.

\textbf{Filtering is not a substitute.} Removing the $K$ least-fair
trajectories instead of editing them does not reproduce the gain --- it
inverts it: $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ falls monotonically with removal
(0.7988 $\to$ 0.7935 at the maximum $K = 2{,}455$), while editing the same
attribution-selected slice raises it to 0.8214. On the $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$
objective, neither selecting (\S\ref{sec:exp-downstream}, most-fair selection control)
nor removing data substitutes for editing it.

\subsection{External Validity: San Francisco}\label{sec:exp-sf}

The entire protocol reruns on San Francisco with no algorithm, metric, or
fidelity change; because $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ is city-specific and
associational (ACS proxies, not hukou), San Francisco tests whether the
\emph{conclusions} reproduce, never whether magnitudes match.

\textbf{Data level.} $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ rises 0.8752 $\to$ 0.9067
($+0.0316$), $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$ $+0.0139$, with Fidelity-A stability and % lint-allow: coincidental value (SF a10 F_spatial delta, not the trim-only F_demo headline)
lift's Fidelity-B cost re-measured on this corpus by the downstream suite
(pending below). Every external instrument improves: Theil $-0.0079$
(CI $[-0.0094, -0.0065]$), and the migrant axis is significant under
\emph{both} grouping conventions on the DP gap (district extremes $-0.0729$,
CI $[-0.1132, -0.0259]$; median split $-0.0370$, CI $[-0.0733, -0.0002]$),
with DI significant under district extremes ($+0.0058$,
CI $[+0.0004, +0.0103]$) and n.s.\ under median split --- a grouping
dependence we state rather than hide (the SF sample is 12 drivers).
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/data/a10/sf12_a10_metrics.json + sf12_a10_external_fairness.json

\textbf{The supply channel replicates; the ratio tension is a tier-1
accounting effect.} The supply channel is again positive and
significant under \emph{both} conventions: $+0.0209$ tier-1
(CI $[+0.0122,$ $+0.0300]$) and $+0.1027$ tier-2
(CI $[+0.0872,$ $+0.1203]$) --- the largest distinct-taxi supply effect of
any corpus in this paper (Shenzhen's three feature sets: $+0.0411$,
$+0.0211$, $+0.0771$; \S\ref{sec:exp-robustness}). But as on the prior
corpus, lift also reroutes \emph{pickups} into under-served cells, raising
recorded demand there: the demand channel is significantly negative
($-0.0533$, CI $[-0.0798,$ $-0.0298]$), and under tier-1 accounting the
total $\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ moves net-negative
($-0.0324$, CI excludes 0). We read the tier-1 net-negative as a
demand-endogeneity finding, not a failed replication: recorded demand in
under-served areas was suppressed, and serving it raises the ratio's
denominator --- the mechanism \S\ref{sec:leveling} predicts, observed in
the wild. The tier-2 recount resolves the tension: counting supply as
distinct taxis rather than fractional presence, the \emph{total} turns
significantly \emph{positive} ($+0.0493$, CI $[+0.0185,$ $+0.0790]$) ---
the real supply improvement outweighs the demand-side denominator increase.
More rides \emph{served} in under-served areas is a demand-side parity gain
--- DP, DI, and Theil all improve on San Francisco --- while the supply
channel independently replicates. The finer spatial partition plausibly
amplifies the effect: San Francisco's demographic units are census tracts,
far smaller than Shenzhen's districts, so the ratio is more sensitive to
lift's rerouted pickups. A ratio-only reading confined to tier-1 accounting
would withhold the SF lift-up claim; we report both accountings, and the
stricter one supports it.

\textbf{San Francisco caveats.} ACS foreign-born share proxies the migrant axis (not
hukou); the 12-driver density-matched subsample is small; 14.9\% of raw
Cabspotting trajectories violate king-move adjacency at source (GPS gaps), so
absolute compliance is unattainable --- the edited corpus is \emph{more}
compliant than raw (87.6\% vs.\ 85.2\%), and the cross-city gate is
edit-relative compliance (100\%); and the GAN-generated source's
distributional collapse is a city-dependent property: the fresh SF run's GAN
stayed healthy, Fidelity-B 0.039, so any collapse-based cautionary note
applies only to the city where it is measured.
