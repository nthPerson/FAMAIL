Imitation-learned mobility policies are trained to reproduce the
trajectories of a real taxi fleet, and those trajectories record the
fleet's demographic service inequity along with its expertise, so a
faithful imitator learns both. Repairing the inequity is a trade-off
between fairness and fidelity: regenerating or heavily rewriting the data
makes it fairer but it stops resembling what real drivers do, while adding
a fairness penalty to one model's training loss leaves the demonstrations
themselves biased for every other model trained on them. We present FATE,
fairness-aware trajectory editing: a data augmentation method that
\emph{edits} a small, attribution-chosen slice of the real trajectories in
place and upweights the edited demonstrations during behavior cloning so
the fairness survives training. Two bounded, gradient-guided editing
phases act under one differentiable objective: \emph{trim} relocates
recorded pickups within over-served areas so their measured over-service
falls, and \emph{lift} reroutes drivers' final seeking minutes into
under-served areas, with the supply consequences of the reroute part of
what the objective optimizes. Every edit stays within a two-cell radius,
and a frozen ST-SiameseNet driver-identity discriminator verifies that an
edited trajectory still reads as its original driver. On taxi trajectories
from two cities (Shenzhen; San Francisco), editing improves established
fairness measures that the objective never optimizes: demographic parity,
disparate impact, and the Theil index. Where demand-only editing improves
such measures purely by reducing over-service, the supply channel opened
by lift adds statistically significant taxi presence, counted as distinct
vehicles, to under-served areas in both cities. Upweighted behavior
cloning carries the fairness gains into trained policies, and does so
specifically through the edited demonstrations: neither a
random-up\-weight\-ing nor a most-fair selection control reproduces the
transfer. A targeted demographic oversampling baseline on Shenzhen
captures only part of the data-level gain at the cost of fabricating
10.5\% of the corpus, and its untargeted placebo degrades fairness
outright. FATE improves measured fairness without fabricating data and
without obscuring which change produced the improvement.
