Imitation-learned mobility policies are trained to reproduce the
trajectories sourced from a real taxi fleet --- and those trajectories record the
fleet's expertise along with its demographic service inequity, so a
faithful imitator learns both. Existing interventions act at two stages
of this pipeline: in-processing penalties constrain the learner while the
demonstrations stay biased, so the objective and the training signal pull
against each other, while synthetic data generation and extensive data
modification can reduce realism, introduce distribution shift, and obscure
the source of any fairness improvement. We present FATE, a
fairness-oriented data augmentation method that instead \emph{edits} a
small, attribution-chosen slice of the real trajectories and upweights the
edited demonstrations so the fairness survives training. The editor
combines two bounded gradient-guided mechanisms under one differentiable
objective: \emph{trim} relocates recorded pickups within over-served areas
so their measured over-service falls, and \emph{lift} reroutes drivers'
final seeking minutes into under-served areas, with the effect on local
supply included in what the objective optimizes. Every edit is confined to
a two-cell radius, with a frozen ST-SiameseNet driver-identity
discriminator as a realism guardrail. On taxi trajectories from two cities (Shenzhen; San
Francisco), editing improves established fairness measures that the
objective never optimizes: demographic parity, disparate impact, and the
Theil index. Where demand-only editing improves such measures purely by
reducing over-service, the supply channel opened by lift adds
statistically significant taxi presence --- counted as distinct vehicles
--- to under-served areas in both cities. Upweighted behavior cloning carries the
fairness-metric gains into trained policies, and does so specifically
through the edited demonstrations: neither a random-up\-weight\-ing nor a
most-fair selection control reproduces the transfer. A targeted
demographic oversampling baseline on Shenzhen captures only part of the
data-level gain at the cost of fabricating 10.5\% of the corpus, and its
untargeted placebo degrades fairness outright. FATE thus improves measured
fairness without fabricating data and without obscuring which change produced
the improvement.
