\section{Derivations}\label{app:derivations}

This appendix carries the closed-form details of $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ deferred
from \S\ref{sec:objective}. With $X$ the z-scored demographic matrix,
$\tilde{X} = [\mathbf{1}\; X]$ its design matrix,
$H = \tilde{X}(\tilde{X}^{\top}\tilde{X})^{-1}\tilde{X}^{\top}$ the \emph{hat
matrix} of the stage-two regression (the projection onto the column space of
$\tilde{X}$ \cite{hoaglinwelsch1978}), and $M$ the centering matrix,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdemo} evaluates in closed form as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fdemo-closed}
F_{\mathrm{demo}}
\;=\; \frac{R^{\top}(I-H)\,R}{R^{\top}M\,R}
\;=\; 1 - r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}} ,
\end{equation}
the stage-two regression evaluated without ever fitting it explicitly: the
numerator is its residual sum of squares, the denominator its total.
% (H-matrix construction moved here from §3.2 on 2026-07-24, Meeting-44 mandate)
This projection form is what
makes the measure usable as an \emph{optimization objective} rather than a
one-shot audit. Demographics are fixed attributes of the cells, so $H$ is a
constant of the optimization: as editing moves $R$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdemo-closed}
implicitly re-fits the demographic regression exactly at every step, with no
coefficients to refresh and nothing trained inside the editing loop. Nor is the
$N \times N$ projection ever materialized: by the normal equations,
$R^{\top}(I-H)R = R^{\top}R -
(\tilde{X}^{\top}R)^{\top}(\tilde{X}^{\top}\tilde{X})^{-1}(\tilde{X}^{\top}R)$,
so each evaluation costs a few length-$N$ inner products.
% src: objective_function/causal_fairness/f_causal_term_reformulation/chosen_formulation/OPTION_B_FORMULATION.md §3.3, §5 (constant H; implicit exact re-fit; differentiability)
% src: famail_temporal/fairness/hat_matrices.py (compact evaluation identity; dense N×N infeasible at production N)
By the Frisch--Waugh--Lovell theorem \cite{frischwaugh1933,lovell1963}, the
residualize-then-project construction recovers exactly the demographic
coefficients of the full multiple regression, so $r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}}$ is
interpretable as the share of demand-adjusted service inequity attributable to
demographics; measuring fairness through the statistical dependence between
protected attributes and outcomes follows the predictability logic of
\cite{feldman2015}, with the direction reversed: there, disparate impact is
certified by predicting the protected attribute from the data; here, the
demographics predict the adjusted service residual.

\textbf{Per-unit deficit partition.} Because $M$ and $I-H$ are idempotent,
the explained share $r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fdemo-closed}
admits the exact per-unit decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-attr}
r^{2}_{\mathrm{demo}}
\;=\; \sum_i \frac{(MR)_i^{2} - \bigl((I-H)R\bigr)_i^{2}}{R^{\top}MR},
\end{equation}
a mathematically exact partition of the fairness deficit across active
units; a signed variant separates over- from under-served units. This is
the demand-deficit attribution that drives trim (\S\ref{sec:attribution}).
% src: PAPER/argument/03_fairness_theory.md (per-cell attribution)

\textbf{Supply-gradient closed form.} The $F_{\mathrm{demo}}$ component of
the value-of-presence map of \S\ref{sec:attribution} has the closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supply-grad}
\frac{\partial F_{\mathrm{demo}}}{\partial S_i}
\;=\; \frac{2}{R^{\top}MR}\;
\frac{\bigl((I-H)R\bigr)_i - F_{\mathrm{demo}}\,(MR)_i}{\max(D_i,\, d_0)},
\end{equation}
against which the automatic gradient is verified.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §4.1 (autograd-verified closed form)

\textbf{Spatial-equity closed form.} The $F_{\mathrm{spatial}}$ term of
\S\ref{sec:objective} scores the inequality of two supply-normalized service
rates --- the demand-service ratio $\mathrm{DSR}_i = D_i / S_i$ and the
arrival-service ratio $\mathrm{ASR}_i = A_i / S_i$, with $A_i$ the unit's
recorded dropoffs and $D_i$, $S_i$ as in \S\ref{sec:problem} --- with the
Gini index, an inequality measure widely used in transportation equity
research \cite{horchergraham2021,karner2024}. The Gini enters as a pairwise
mean absolute difference, differentiable almost everywhere and hence usable
inside the gradient objective:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fspatial}
\begin{split}
\mathrm{Gini}(x) &\;=\; \frac{\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\,\lvert x_i - x_j\rvert}
{2N^{2}\,\bar{x}},\\
F_{\mathrm{spatial}} &\;=\; 1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\mathrm{Gini}(\mathrm{DSR})
+ \mathrm{Gini}(\mathrm{ASR})\bigr).
\end{split}
\end{equation}

% src: famail_temporal/fairness/spatial.py (DSR/ASR ratios; sorted-identity evaluation, no N² materialization)

\section{Editor Implementation Details}\label{app:editor}

\textbf{Grid configuration.} Both cities discretize space into
$0.01^{\circ}$ grid cells and hourly time blocks (\S\ref{sec:problem}):
a $48{\times}90$ cell grid on Shenzhen and a $32{\times}30$ grid on San
Francisco. The resolution is an implementation choice; the formulation
requires only a discrete partition of space and time.
% src: PAPER/argument/02_datasets.md (0.01° grid, hourly blocks, per-city dims)
% (moved here from §3.1/§4.1 on 2026-07-24 — Meeting-44 [1:29:27]/[1:37:28]:
% resolution to appendix, defined once)

\textbf{Soft cell assignment.} The discrete grid is bridged by
tem\-per\-a\-ture-annealed soft cell assignment
\cite{jang2017gumbel,maddison2017concrete,bengio2013ste} over the same
$5{\times}5$ window used to aggregate supply, moving from broad early
exploration to precise late assignment; the best iterate is kept.

\textbf{Editor constants.} In lift, the pickup moves by the full offset
$\delta$ and earlier tail states move by linearly tapered fractions
$w_j = j/\ell$ ($0.25/0.5/0.75/1.0$ at $\ell = 4$), with the anchor state
before the tail fixed. Each moved seeking state contributes $1/12$ of an hourly
presence unit to each cell of its $5{\times}5$ neighborhood in the supply
perturbation $\Delta S$, and the objective evaluates supply as
$\mathrm{clamp}(S + \Delta S,\, s_0)$ with supply floor $s_0 = 0.1$.
% src: PAPER/supply-lift/LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §3, §5-6 (taper, presence mass, floors, live fidelity tail)

\textbf{King-move repair.} The source data is preprocessed --- following the
pipeline of \cite{zhang2022cgail} --- to satisfy a king-move rule: consecutive
states move at most one cell per axis, $\max(|dx|,|dy|) \le 1$. Edits should
not be held to a lower standard than the data they edit; a bounded two-cell
pickup move would silently violate the very rule the source trajectories are
filtered on. Discretized edits therefore pass through an exact
backward-reachability repair that returns the king-compliant assignment
nearest the tapered targets, or reports that none exists.
% src: LIFT_ALGORITHM_REFERENCE.md §3 (king-move rule), §6 (repair; lift skips in-loop)

\textbf{Linearized-offset screen.} The candidate screen of
\S\ref{sec:attribution}, in detail. Each trajectory's tail --- its last
$\ell$ seeking states and pickup --- is rigidly translated by every nonzero
integer offset $\delta \in [-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]^{2}$, and each
translation is scored by its linearized gain: the value-of-presence
collected at the tail's new positions minus that at its old ones, each state
weighted by the presence mass it carries and the map summed over the same
$5{\times}5$ window through which presence spreads (\S\ref{sec:problem}). A
trajectory's score is its best offset.

\textbf{Perturbation-arm stationarity.} Detail for the
baselines of \S\ref{sec:exp-baselines}. We pre-registered
$\delta = 0$-initialized ablation arms expecting a no-op --- a
difference-mode Siamese head has zero subgradient at an identical pair ---
but the deployed discriminator compares embeddings by \emph{concatenation},
which is not stationary there: measured, the $\delta = 0$ arms attack rather
than stall (the iterative arm lands within $0.0003$ of its random-start
twin; the single-step arm differs with initialization, as a one-step method
must). The stationarity assumption held only for the head we did not deploy.
% src: famail_temporal/results/2026-07-13T06-26-19/…06-27-50 arm metrics (nors); deployed checkpoint combination_mode="concatenation" (fidelity/model.py); gradient-at-zero verified live 2026-07-13

\textbf{The two realism axes.} \emph{Fidelity-A} is the identity axis: the
frozen driver-identity discriminator's mean score on matched pairs (an
edited trajectory against a trace of its original driver; higher is
better), the evaluation-time reading of the \S\ref{sec:objective}
guardrail, and validated by its separation from mismatched-driver pairs.
\emph{Fidelity-B} is the distributional axis: a Jensen--Shannon divergence
over aggregate mobility statistics, computed against the raw corpus (raw
scores 0); lower is better.

\section{Statistical Protocol Details}\label{app:protocol}

\textbf{Exact-Wilcoxon floor arithmetic.} With $n = 6$ paired seeds the
two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$ floors at $0.03125$, attained exactly
when all six seeds share a sign, which is why $p = 0.031$ reads only as a
sign-unanimity certificate. The flagship w30 comparison at $n = 12$ paired
seeds has floor $.00049$, low enough for an exact Wilcoxon $p$ to survive
multiple-comparison correction. Suites with $n = 5$ seeds cannot reach
$p < 0.05$ (floor $0.0625$) and are reported as effect-versus-noise.
% src: PAPER/argument/04_evaluation.md §3; PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §1, §5

\textbf{Why the bootstrap resamples units.} A driver-level bootstrap is
unavailable rather than declined: the demand grid is a mean-hourly artifact,
not a per-driver sum, and supply is environmental, so $Y$ does not decompose
into driver contributions. Active grid units are the only available
resampling level.
% src: PAPER/external-metrics/FINDINGS.md §5

\textbf{Instrument definitions.} The service ratio $Y$ is grouped
per equity axis (housing price, compensation, migrant share) under two
conventions, district extremes and median split. Demographic parity (DP) is
the signed gap between the group levels
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{disadv.})$ and
$\mathrm{mean}(Y \mid \mathrm{adv.})$; disparate impact (DI) is their ratio
(1 = parity); the Theil index is between-region and grouping independent.

\textbf{Fairness interventions in machine learning.} They group into
pre-, in-, and post-processing \cite{barocas2023}; the pre-processing
instruments FATE builds on are instance reweighing \cite{kamirancalders2012}
and disparate-impact repair \cite{feldman2015}, both built for labeled
classification data. Among formal definitions \cite{vermarubin2018}, FATE
targets conditional statistical parity \cite{corbettdavies2017}. As
formulated, none operate on sequential demonstrations or score fairness
over a spatial service distribution. FATE moves reweighing onto edited
demonstrations, and the parity condition onto a continuous spatial service
ratio used as a differentiable editing objective.

\textbf{Fairness in urban transportation.} Transportation equity measures
service distributions with the Gini index \cite{horchergraham2021,karner2024}
and entropy- or welfare-based indices \cite{theil1967,atkinson1970,demaio2007}.
The closest modeling neighbor regularizes a ride-hailing demand predictor at
training time, shrinking a racial prediction-error gap from 0.361 to 0.084
\cite{zheng2023};
% verified: paper/reviews/2026-07-12-prose-citation-audit.md (zheng2023 Table 2, Conv-LSTM arm)
that correction lives inside one model's loss and does not transfer to any
other consumer of the data. FATE addresses the same bias one stage earlier,
on the demonstrations, and is scored on equity measures its objective never
optimizes.

\textbf{Imitation learning for mobility, and mobility as identity.}
Conditional GAIL variants recover taxi-driver strategies from trajectories
\cite{zhang2019cgail,zhang2022cgail}, explainable extensions expose the
recovered policies \cite{pan2020xgail}, and neural simulators generate human
mobility \cite{feng2020simulate}; this line optimizes realism and never
audits the demographic fairness of the imitated allocation. A parallel line
establishes that mobility is highly identifying: trajectory-user linking
\cite{gao2017tuler,zhou2018tulvae,miao2020deeptul} and Siamese verification
of seeking traces \cite{ren2020stsiamese}. FATE contributes no new generator
or imitation algorithm; it edits the demonstrations such models consume, and
repurposes the identity-verification line as a frozen fidelity guardrail,
avoiding the instability of a live adversarial game \cite{hoermon2016}.

\textbf{Adversarial perturbation and recourse.} The editing machinery
descends from bounded signed-gradient perturbations
\cite{goodfellow2015fgsm,kurakin2017ifgsm} instantiated spatio-temporally
\cite{hu2023stifgsm}, with discrete structure bridged by straight-through
estimation \cite{bengio2013ste} and continuous relaxation
\cite{jang2017gumbel,maddison2017concrete}. Algorithmic recourse reads the
same counterfactual tooling constructively \cite{ustun2019recourse,
wachter2018counterfactual}, but only to flip one individual's decision, with
no population-level fairness target and no data-realism obligation. FATE
extends the constructive reading from the individual to the population, and
the $\varepsilon$-bound is reinterpreted from an attack-stealth budget to an
identity-preservation budget.

\textbf{Leveling down and feedback loops.} The leveling-down objection
(equality achieved by making the better-off worse rather than the worse-off
better) originates in the ethics of equality \cite{parfit1997}, has been
applied to machine-learning fairness constraints \cite{mittelstadt2024}, and
data augmentation is the one intervention class observed to help the
disadvantaged group instead \cite{zietlow2022}. Feedback-loop analyses show
that allocation systems trained on their own historical output entrench a
self-reinforcing bias \cite{ensign2018,lumisaac2016}. Both critiques shape
this paper: trim's demand-only editing is an analogue of leveling down
(\S\ref{sec:leveling}), lift exists expressly to level up, and demand
endogeneity bounds what any demand-adjusted metric can see.
