\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% Drafted 2026-07-12 per docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-12-paper-prose-sections-design.md.
% No numeric values by design (restatement mirrors §1 contributions qualitatively).

FATE makes fairness a property of the demonstrations rather than of a
constrained model or a synthetic corpus: it edits a small slice of real
trajectories, chosen by attribution, under one bounded, realism-guarded
objective --- trimming advantaged over-service and lifting drivers' final
seeking minutes into under-served areas with the supply consequences of the
reroute differentiable and endogenous --- then upweights the edited
demonstrations so the fairness survives imitation. On two cities
the edits improve established fairness measures the objective never
optimizes, and, unlike the demand-only editor whose gains never reached the
under-served group, the supply channel adds statistically significant taxi
presence to that group. Upweighted behavior cloning carries those gains into
trained policies, and the control arms confirm the edit itself --- not
oversampling, not selection --- is what carries them.

The claims come with stated bounds. The optimized fairness term is
associational: a partial $R^2$ over roughly ten district-level demographic
profiles, with no identification and no individual-level reading.
Adjusting for recorded demand treats demand as exogenous, but demand is
suppressed exactly where service has been historically thin, so a
demand-adjusted metric under-detects the inequity it audits --- the same
censoring that bounds any demand-side editor. With five or six paired
seeds, no single test survives multiple-comparison correction; the one
comparison extended to twelve paired seeds in each city --- the flagship
w30 recovery --- does ($p = .00049$, 12/12 positive in both), and the
remaining downstream evidence rests on effect direction and magnitude,
confidence intervals, a monotone dose-response, and null controls rather
than on any uncorrected p-value.

Two directions follow naturally. The first is the single-pass editor
\S\ref{sec:editor} anticipates, in which supply is endogenous for every edit
and the gradient decides each edit's character. The second is transfer:
whether other imitation objectives and adversarially trained generators
inherit the edited corpus's fairness as upweighted behavior cloning does,
whether training-side allocation constraints can close the rollout gap that
data editing alone only attenuates, and whether the recipe holds on further
cities.
