\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% Drafted 2026-07-12 per docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-12-paper-prose-sections-design.md.
% No numeric values by design (restatement mirrors §1 contributions qualitatively).

FAMAIL makes fairness a property of the demonstrations. Rather than
constraining a model or generating a synthetic corpus, it edits a small,
attribution-targeted slice of real trajectories under one bounded,
realism-guarded objective --- trimming the measured over-service of
advantaged areas, and lifting drivers' final seeking minutes into
under-served areas with the supply consequences of the reroute
differentiable and endogenous --- then upweights the edited demonstrations
so the fairness survives imitation. On two cities the edits improve established fairness
measures the objective never optimizes and, unlike the demand-only editor
whose gains never touched the under-served group, the supply channel adds
statistically significant taxi presence to that group; upweighted behavior
cloning carries the fairness-metric gains into trained policies, and the control arms
confirm the edit itself --- not oversampling, not selection --- is what
carries them.

The claims come with stated bounds. The optimized fairness term is
associational: a partial $R^2$ over roughly ten district-level demographic
profiles, with no identification and no individual-level reading.
Adjusting for recorded demand treats demand as exogenous, but demand is
suppressed exactly where service has been historically thin, so a
demand-adjusted metric under-detects the inequity it audits --- the same
censoring that bounds any demand-side editor. With five or six paired
seeds, no single test survives multiple-comparison correction; the one
comparison extended to twelve paired seeds in each city --- the flagship
w30 recovery --- does ($p = .00049$, 12/12 positive in both), and the
remaining downstream evidence rests on effect direction and magnitude,
confidence intervals, a monotone dose-response, and null controls rather
than on any uncorrected p-value.

not fine-grained trajectory-shape realism.

Two directions follow naturally. The editor's two-phase structure --- trim
first, then lift --- is a scientific control, so the immediate extension is the editor
\S\ref{sec:editor} anticipates: a single pass in which supply is
endogenous for every edit and the gradient decides each edit's character.
The second direction is transfer: whether other imitation objectives and
adversarially trained generators inherit the edited corpus's fairness the
way upweighted behavior cloning does, whether allocation constraints on
the training side can close the rollout gap that data editing alone only
attenuates, and whether the recipe holds on further cities.
