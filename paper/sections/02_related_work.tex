\textbf{Fairness interventions in machine learning.} Algorithmic fairness
interventions are conventionally grouped by where they act: pre-processing
methods repair the training data, in-processing methods constrain the
learner, and post-processing methods adjust its outputs
\cite{barocas2023}. FAMAIL belongs to the pre-processing
family, whose canonical instruments --- instance reweighing
\cite{kamirancalders2012} and disparate-impact repair of feature
distributions \cite{feldman2015} --- were built for labeled classification
data. Among the many formal fairness definitions \cite{vermarubin2018},
the notion FAMAIL targets is conditional statistical parity
\cite{corbettdavies2017}: disparities count only after permitted factors
are adjusted for. Both instruments reappear here in unfamiliar roles ---
reweighing applied to demonstrations rather than labeled examples, and the
parity condition transplanted from a classifier's predictions to a
continuous spatial service ratio and made a differentiable editing
objective.

\textbf{Fairness in urban transportation.} Transportation equity has its
own measurement tradition, in which the Gini index over service
distributions is the most widely used instrument
\cite{horchergraham2021,karner2024}, alongside entropy- and
welfare-based indices \cite{theil1967,atkinson1970,demaio2007}. On the
modeling side, the closest neighbor to this work regularizes a ride-hailing
demand predictor in-processing, shrinking a racial prediction-error gap
from 0.361 to 0.084 \cite{zheng2023}. FAMAIL addresses the same
% verified: paper/reviews/2026-07-12-prose-citation-audit.md (zheng2023 Table 2, Conv-LSTM arm)
demand-model bias one stage earlier --- on the demonstrations the model
trains on, rather than inside its loss --- and is scored on equity
measures its objective never optimizes.

\textbf{Imitation learning for mobility, and mobility as identity.}
Imitation learning is an established route to urban mobility models:
conditional GAIL variants recover taxi-driver strategies from trajectories
\cite{zhang2019cgail,zhang2022cgail}, explainable extensions expose the
recovered policies \cite{pan2020xgail}, and neural simulators generate
human mobility wholesale \cite{feng2020simulate}. That literature
optimizes realism; the demographic fairness of the imitated allocation is
not among its objectives. A parallel line establishes that individual
mobility is highly identifying: trajectory-user linking recovers who
generated a trace \cite{gao2017tuler,zhou2018tulvae,miao2020deeptul}, and
Siamese verification decides whether two seeking traces share a driver
\cite{ren2020stsiamese}. FAMAIL contributes no new generator or imitation
algorithm; it edits the demonstrations such models consume, and it
repurposes the identity-verification line as a frozen realism guardrail
--- avoiding the instability of a live adversarial game
\cite{hoermon2016}.

\textbf{Adversarial perturbation and recourse.} The editing machinery
descends from adversarial examples: bounded signed-gradient perturbations
\cite{goodfellow2015fgsm,kurakin2017ifgsm}, instantiated
spatio-temporally for mobility signatures by ST-iFGSM \cite{hu2023stifgsm},
with discrete structure bridged by continuous relaxation
\cite{jang2017gumbel,maddison2017concrete} and straight-through gradient
estimation \cite{bengio2013ste}. Algorithmic
recourse established that the same perturbation tooling built for
counterfactuals can prescribe beneficial change rather than attack
\cite{ustun2019recourse,wachter2018counterfactual} --- for one
individual's feature vector, to flip one decision. FAMAIL extends that
constructive reading from the individual to the population: the bounded
perturbation edits real trajectories to reduce demographic service
inequity, and the $\varepsilon$-bound is reinterpreted from an
attack-stealth budget to a budget for preserving driver identity.

\textbf{Leveling down and feedback loops.} Two critiques frame what a
fairness gain should mean. The leveling-down objection --- equality
achieved by making the better-off worse rather than the worse-off better
--- originates in the ethics of equality \cite{parfit1997} and has been
pressed concretely against machine-learning fairness constraints
\cite{mittelstadt2024}, with data augmentation observed to be the one
intervention class that helps the disadvantaged group instead
\cite{zietlow2022}. Feedback-loop analyses show that allocation systems
trained on their own historical output entrench a self-reinforcing
allocation bias \cite{ensign2018,lumisaac2016} --- in our domain, the
under-service of the neighborhoods that were under-served to begin with. Both critiques are load-bearing in this
paper: we show our own demand-only editor closes gaps only from the top ---
an analogue of leveling down
(\S\ref{sec:leveling}) --- build the supply channel expressly to lift up, and
state demand endogeneity as the bound on what any demand-adjusted metric
can see.
